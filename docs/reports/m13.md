projection rather than a measurement it says so, in the same sentence.

## 1. `_repair` — the reproduction, and what it actually was

### The reproduction

M11 found it on `PRSNT`, the A1-to-B17 presence-detect strap that
`examples/pcie-sata` deliberately leaves off the netlist because routing it means
crossing all three lane pairs. The strap was briefly on the board, the second-pass
repair routed it straight through two `REFCLK` tracks, and KiCad reported two
`tracks_crossing` errors. M11 could not explain it: *the copper it crossed was in
the obstacle set.*

Putting the strap back reproduces it exactly. On the tree as it stood before this
milestone:

```
kicad-cli pcb drc, examples/pcie-sata + the A1-B17 strap
  tracks_crossing: 2
  PRSNT on B.Cu overlaps REFCLKN on B.Cu by 0.0584 mm² at (138.648, 134.273)
  PRSNT on B.Cu overlaps REFCLKP on B.Cu by 0.0584 mm² at (138.216, 134.374)
```

That is `tests/test_routing.py::TestRepairDoesNotCrossAnotherNet`, which builds the
strap into a copy of the example, routes it, and asserts both that the new
invariant finds nothing and that KiCad's own DRC reports no `tracks_crossing`. It
fails on the pre-M13 tree and passes now.

### What it was

The copper was in the obstacle *list* and not in the obstacle *set*.

Finished copper accumulates in a `list[Obstacle]`. The free-space calculation wants
a dict keyed by obstacle name, and both `geometry_for` and `_repair` built one the
same way:

```python
environment = replace(base, obstacles=dict(base.obstacles))
for obstacle in placed:
    environment.obstacles[obstacle.name] = obstacle   # <- silently lossy
```

Two pieces of copper that share a name become one, and a dict assignment cannot
fail. A differential pair split across two layers by a via transition produces one
`RoutedConnection` per layer, and `route/pairs.py` names each one's coupled leg
after the same two pair terminals — `REFCLKN<` to `REFCLKN>` — so the F.Cu segment
overwrote the B.Cu one. Measured on the shipped example, with no strap on it at all:

```
duplicate obstacle names in `placed`, examples/pcie-sata:  4
  track:REFCLKN/REFCLKN<>REFCLKN>#0        (B.Cu and F.Cu)
  track:REFCLKP/REFCLKP<>REFCLKP>#0        (B.Cu and F.Cu)
  track:PCIE_RXN/PCIE_RXN<>PCIE_RXN>#0     (B.Cu and F.Cu)
  track:PCIE_RXP/PCIE_RXP<>PCIE_RXP>#0     (B.Cu and F.Cu)
```

Four pieces of copper had been invisible to every route realized after them since
M11c landed. `pcie-sata` got away with it because nothing else needed to be there.
`PRSNT` needed to be exactly there.

**This is not a defect in `_repair`.** `geometry_for` is shared with primary
routing, so primary routing had the same hole; the repair was simply the pass that
happened to be sent through it. Saying so matters, because "fix the repair path"
would have left the hole open. The guardrail said the repair path and the invariant
only, and anything wider was to be stopped and asked about — this is not wider, it
is the same defect one level down, and the fix touches no routing *decision*: it
adds copper to an obstacle set that was losing it.

### The fix, in three parts

1. **`route/geometry.py::with_copper`** merges finished copper into the environment
   without loss: a name that is already taken is *suffixed* rather than
   overwritten. Copper can only ever be added, whatever it is called, so the
   invariant is a property of the merge rather than of everybody's naming
   discipline. `geometry_for` and `_repair` both go through it.
2. **`_accept` names track obstacles by layer as well** —
   `track:{net}@{layer}/{start}>{end}` — so the collision does not arise in the
   first place. Obstacle names never reach a file, so this changes no output.
3. **The repair charges its route to the shared field.** `_repair` searched a
   private field and never booked its demand against the negotiation's congestion
   accounting, so every route that searched afterwards saw a corridor that looked
   emptier than it was. `_charge` now runs on the repaired route, which is the
   "same bookkeeping as primary routing" the milestone asked for.

### The invariant

`route/invariant.py` asks the finished board the blunt question: does any two nets'
copper overlap, on a layer they share or through a via barrel. It runs at the end of
every `route_board` and reports `route-nets-cross` as an **error** — copper of two
nets in one place is a short circuit, and there is no board on which that is a
judgement call.

It is deliberately about *intersection*, not clearance: KiCad's DRC already measures
clearance, against the filled board, with the fabricator's rules, and a second worse
copy here would produce a different number from the one that matters. Overlap needs
no rules to be wrong.

Cost, measured on the three densest examples:

| example | routing | invariant | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| `pcie-sata` (172 legs, 42 vias) | 35.49 s | **9.0 ms** | 0.025 % |
| `qfn-fanout` (56 legs, 30 vias) | 5.68 s | **5.3 ms** | 0.09 % |
| `mcu-4layer` (49 legs, 5 vias) | 10.22 s | **2.9 ms** | 0.03 % |

Cheap enough that "debug/CI level is fine" was not a trade worth making. It runs
always.

All eleven examples come back with zero crossings, before and after the fix — the
defect was latent, exactly as M11 said.

## 2. The impedance model

### What M12 found, and one correction to how it was written down

Every simulated link on `examples/pcie-sata` came back below its declared target,
because both closed forms this project carries describe a **bare** microstrip and
every board it builds pours ground up to its pairs at the class clearance.

`m12.md` and `docs/roadmap.md` both concluded that the derived widths are
"systematically **narrow** for the boards it actually builds". They are
systematically **wide**. Impedance falls as a trace widens, so a board reading
*below* its target was built too wide, and correcting the model has to narrow it.
The direction is now asserted in a test
(`test_the_coplanar_model_derives_a_narrower_trace`) rather than left in prose that
was pointing the wrong way.

### The model, and the one that was measured and rejected

Full reasoning in [ADR 0012](../decisions/0012-coplanar-impedance.md). In one
paragraph: the shipped model is IPC-2141's *own* edge-coupling term applied once per
neighbouring conductor and superposed as partial capacitances — the pair's other
half on one side, the pour on the other, which is Ghione & Naldi's decomposition
applied to the closed form this project already derives and audits with. It reduces
to exactly today's answer with one neighbour, so no board without a pour beside its
pairs moves at all.

The published conductor-backed-CPW conformal map is implemented too
(`grounded_cpw`), because it is what the decision was measured against. Across the
twelve links M12 left behind:

| model | mean error | RMS | worst |
|---|---|---|---|
| bare microstrip, what M11 shipped | **+34.8 %** | 39.6 % | +67.0 % |
| Wadell/Simons conductor-backed CPW, odd mode | **+61.7 %** | 66.0 % | +107.6 % |
| the same, as a ratio against its own far limit | **+17.5 %** | 23.9 % | +47.0 % |
| **partial capacitances — what ships** | **+7.0 %** | **16.3 %** | +34.5 % |

The conformal form's far limit tends to `εr` rather than to a microstrip's
effective permittivity, so it reads 109.6 Ω for an isolated trace where IPC-2141
reads 89.6. Inside its own domain it is good — 48.4 Ω on the standard 50 Ω RO4350
conductor-backed CPW — and that is asserted as a test so the ADR's claim stays
checkable.

### The widths that changed

`examples/pcie-sata` is the only bundled example with an `impedance_diff_ohm`; the
other ten declare none and nothing about their geometry moves.

| class | target | model | pour gap | width before | width after | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `pcie_tx` | 85 Ω | `cpwg` | 0.15 mm | 0.2888 mm | **0.1846 mm** | −36 % |
| `pcie_rx` | 85 Ω | `cpwg` | 0.15 mm | 0.2888 mm | **0.1846 mm** | −36 % |
| `sata` | 100 Ω | `cpwg` | 0.15 mm | 0.2390 mm | **0.1379 mm** | −42 % |

Which model each class used is published rather than inferred: it is on
`ImpedanceTarget`, it reaches `check`'s JSON under `summary.highspeed.classes`, and
`hs-impedance-model` says it in prose with the geometry and the pour gap beside it.

### Three things a narrower pair broke, and none of them was in the impedance code

The re-derived widths inverted an assumption `route/pairs.py` had been relying on
and no board had ever stated: that a class's single-ended `trace_width_mm` is
*narrower* than its pair's. `examples/pcie-sata`'s own comment says so explicitly —
"deliberately narrower than the pair's" — and at 0.2 mm against a 0.1379 mm pair it
stopped being true.

* **Every pair on the board refused to couple**, with `pair fan-out X: the end is
  not in the routable area`. `_fan_out` finds the coupled run's hand-over point in
  the *pair's* free space and then tightens the fan-out to it in the *fan-out's*,
  which is a different width and therefore a different free space. While the pair
  was the wider of the two the pair's view was the stricter and this was free.
  `_junction_span` now trims the run's two ends back to where the fan-out can
  actually reach them — only the ends, because the interior is legal at the pair's
  own width by construction and trimming it would shorten the coupled length for
  nothing.
* **Twelve clearance violations, every one a pair against itself**, at 0.1423 mm
  against a 0.15 mm rule. The two halves are offset from one centre-line at the
  pair's pitch, so a fan-out drawn wider than the pair eats the gap that pitch
  exists to hold. A fan-out is now clamped to the pair's width, which states the
  invariant instead of relying on the source to keep it and changes nothing on a
  board that already did.
* **And clamping it broke a fourth thing, which is the interesting one.** Four
  separate measurements had been *inferring* which leg of a pair is the coupled run
  and which is the fan-out, by comparing the leg's width to the pair's: the
  uncoupled-length budget, the wall-hugging scan, which copper is fed back between
  the two halves, and which leg a meander may be folded into. `_uncoupled_lengths`
  even documented the inference — *"the legs say which is which without anything
  having to remember"*. Clamping the fan-out to the pair's width made the two
  equal, so all four silently stopped distinguishing them: the budget read zero on
  every pair, and the two halves' fan-outs stopped seeing each other as obstacles.
  Caught by re-measuring the board rather than by a test. `StretchResult` now
  carries a `coupled` flag and the four sites read it.

All four are in the pair machinery M11d wrote, all four were found by re-measuring
the board rather than by a test, and all four are now what the board's own DRC
checks. The fourth is the one worth remembering: an inference that had been true on
every board for two milestones is not the same thing as an invariant, and the
cheapest way to find out which you have is to change the geometry underneath it.

### What the board does now

| | before M13 | after M13 |
|---|---|---|
| connections routed | 90 / 90 | **90 / 90** |
| pairs coupled | 12 / 12 | **12 / 12** |
| vias | 44 | **42** |
| copper | 1091.9 mm | **1108.1 mm** (+1.5 %) |
| DRC errors on the filled board | 0 | **0** |
| cross-net overlaps | 0 | **0** |
| `check` warnings | 10 | **6** |

The two fewer vias are M13a's doing, not M13b's: two routes that used to be able to
sit on top of copper nobody could see now go round it.

**Skew got worse, and this is the part worth reading twice.**

| pair | skew before | skew after | budget | as delay |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` | 0.000 mm | **0.191 mm** over | 0.125 mm | 1.2 ps |
| `PCIE_TXN_C+PCIE_TXP_C` | 0.000 mm | **0.247 mm** over | 0.125 mm | 1.5 ps |
| `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` | 0.256 mm over | **0.219 mm** over | 0.125 mm | 1.4 ps |
| `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | 0.292 mm over | **0.359 mm** over | 0.125 mm | 2.2 ps |
| the eight `sata` pairs | 0.004–0.199 mm | **0.000–0.041 mm** | 0.25 mm | ≤ 0.3 ps |

M11 delivered two PCIe pairs over budget; this board delivers four. The SATA pairs
all improved, several to zero. The cause is not the model but the *geometry*: a
0.185 mm pair at a 0.15 mm gap leaves its pads on a different line from a 0.289 mm
one, the fan-out at each end is asymmetric by a different amount, and the meander
pass is refused on all four for lack of room in the fan-out's own corridor —
`diff-pair-meander-refused`, with the millimetres, on each.

In picoseconds it is 1.2–2.2 ps against a 125 ps Gen3 unit interval, which is the
same order M11 argued was absorbable and M12 independently corroborated. It is
still a regression on the flagship board, it went the wrong way, and calling it
anything else would be the kind of quiet reframing this project exists not to do.
The right fix is placement — moving the end components so the two halves break out
symmetrically, which is what the finding's own hint says — and that is a design
change M13 had no mandate to make.

### The simulation, and the mesh that had to be fixed first

The mesh is the sharpest thing M13b found, and it has nothing to do with impedance.
M12 calibrated a fixed 50 µm cell on `examples/mcu-4layer`'s 0.25 mm pair at a
0.2 mm gap. On the 0.185 mm pair at a 0.15 mm gap the coplanar model derives, that
same mesh is **silently catastrophic**. Sweeping the cell size on one slice and
changing nothing else:

| cell | x grid lines | Zdiff read |
|---|---|---|
| 50 µm — M12's default | 47 | **12.2 Ω** |
| 45 µm | 61 | **48.2 Ω** |
| 40 µm | 64 | 74.5 Ω |
| 35 µm | 72 | 74.5 Ω |
| 30 µm | — | 76.7 Ω |
| 25 µm | 112 | 78.9 Ω |

Every one of those runs decayed to −59 dB or better, exited zero, and reported no
warning. **The 50 µm answer is wrong by a factor of seven and looks exactly as
trustworthy as the 25 µm one.** That is the failure mode ADR 0011 phase 0 named — a
confident wrong answer at a zero exit code — arriving through the mesh this time,
and a fixed cell size cannot catch it, because whether 50 µm is fine depends on the
geometry being meshed.

`si/inputs.py::grid_optimal_um` now derives the cell from the slice: six cells
across the trace, five across the gap, never coarser than the setting. The cliff
above sits at 4.1 and 3.3. On `pcie-sata` that is 30.7 µm for the PCIe classes and
22.9 µm for SATA, and on `mcu-4layer` — the board M12 calibrated on — **41.6 µm**
rather than the declared 50. That last one is worth stating plainly because the
first draft of this report claimed the rule left `mcu-4layer` alone and a test
proved otherwise: 41.6 µm is inside the 25–100 µm band M12's own sweep covered on
that pair, over which the answer moved 5.3 %, so the calibration still bounds what
runs, but the default is not untouched. `max_steps` follows the mesh for
the same reason (`si/inputs.py::max_steps`): an FDTD timestep is set by the cell, so
a fixed limit does not make a finer run cheaper, it makes it *stop earlier*.

### Old against new, per link

The three PCIe links, on the board this milestone ships, against the same links as
M12 measured them:

| Link | Target | M12 — microstrip width | M13 — coplanar width | Change | Inside ±10 %? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` | 85 Ω | 66.6 Ω (**−21.6 %**) | **80.0 Ω (−5.8 %)** | error cut to a quarter | **yes** |
| `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` | 85 Ω | 61.5 Ω (**−27.6 %**) | **72.8 Ω (−14.4 %)** | error roughly halved | no |
| `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | 85 Ω | 50.9 Ω (**−40.1 %**) | **49.8 Ω (−41.4 %)** | unchanged | no |
| `SATA*` × 8 | 100 Ω | 62.5–91.9 Ω (−37.5 to −8.1 %) | **not re-simulated** | — | — |

**One of three meets the acceptance, and the band was not widened to make the other
two.** What the residuals are and what is thought to cause them:

* **`REFCLK` did not converge, and its number means nothing.** Its run stopped with
  energy at **−12 dB**, having run its step limit out — the slice is a resonant
  cavity, which M12 already recorded for this pair at −41 dB and which the finer
  mesh made worse by needing more steps for the same physical settling. A number
  read off a run with 6 % of its energy still bouncing around is not a measurement
  of a trace, and the fact that it barely moved (50.9 → 49.8 Ω) while the trace
  narrowed by 36 % is itself evidence that it is reading the cavity rather than the
  line. **Hypothesis:** the step limit, not the model. It is testable — raise
  `max_steps` for that class and watch the energy floor — and it was not tested here.
* **`PCIE_RX` at −14.4 % is the one real residual.** It changes layer through a via
  transition, so its two ends are referenced to different planes and the reported
  impedance is the whole link rather than either half — the slicer says so in its
  own note, and a median taken across two cross-sections joined by a via barrel is
  not a characteristic impedance. **Hypothesis:** part model error and part
  apples-to-oranges. Separating them means simulating one half at a time, which the
  slicer does not do.
* **`PCIE_TX` meets it but is flagged unusable.** `|Sdd21|` reaches 1.22 across its
  whole band — a systematic excess, not a spike — where `PCIE_RX` on the same board
  at the same cell size stays at 0.85–0.99. Both decayed past −60 dB, so it is not
  truncation. The tool refuses to publish an impedance verdict for it, which is
  correct behaviour and not an explanation. The transmit link is the one whose two
  coupling capacitors the slicer bridges into copper; that is the suspect, not the
  finding. Under "Open questions".

### The eight SATA links, and why they did not run

Measured rather than estimated. One SATA slice was meshed and started under the new
rule: **3.63 M cells at a 22.9 µm cell, with `max_steps` at 87 336** against M12's
3.41 M cells at 40 000. The cell count barely moves — gerber2ems refines only near
copper, and the slice is mostly empty — but the step count does, by 2.18×. Two
independent readings of the cost:

* from the solver's own throughput on this machine (800–1200 MC/s):
  **9–13 minutes a link, 1.2–1.8 hours for eight**;
* from M12's own measured wall clocks scaled by cells × steps:
  **2 h 11 min × 2.32 ≈ 5 hours for eight**.

Somewhere between those. It was not spent, and the milestone's own guardrail is the
reason: an honest halt beats a number nobody can stand behind, and what was left was
enough time to run *either* the eight SATA links *or* the acceptance suite and the
report, not both. The SATA links are the larger deferral and they are the ones that
would have gone unwitnessed.

The consequence is stated where it hurts: **M13c's fitted verdict has run on three
links, not eleven.**

## 3. The skew verdict

### What was built

M12 asked whether simulation discriminates the two pairs M11 delivered over their
skew budget. Through the published scalar the answer was **no, and worse than no**:
the three links `mode_conversion` flagged were the three *best*-matched pairs on the
board, and both mismatched ones passed. The cause was not that the physics is
invisible — `REFCLKP/N` tracked `|sin(π f Δτ)|` to within 3.2 dB across its band —
it was that a worst-in-band maximum reads the **floor**, and the floor varied by
more than 25 dB between pairs on one board while the skew moved it by about 3 dB.

`si/results.py::fit_skew` fits

```
|Scd21|² = (|Sdd21|² + |Scd21|²) · sin²(π f Δτ) + floor²
```

across the band, recovering Δτ from the *shape* rather than from the level. The
amplitude is measured rather than fitted: a pair with skew Δτ transmits
`cos(π f Δτ)` differentially and converts `sin(π f Δτ)`, so the two together are
what the line delivered, and letting the fit choose that lets a lossy link and a big
skew trade against each other. For any candidate Δτ the best flat floor is a closed
form, so what is left is a one-dimensional scan on a fixed grid — no seed, no
tolerance, the same answer on any machine. The fitted delay is turned into a length
with the pair's *own* measured ps/mm, so the comparison against `max_skew_mm` uses
no constant the two verification layers had to agree on beforehand.

Three verdicts, and the third is the one a scalar cannot express: **pass**,
**warn**, and **under-floor** — the skew term never rises far enough above the
fitted floor for the delay to be a measurement, and the number returned is an
**upper bound**.

Unit-tested against synthetic curves where the answer is known exactly: 1, 2, 5 and
10 ps are recovered to within 0.05 ps; a curve with no skew fits no skew; a 0.05 ps
skew under a −25 dB floor comes back as an upper bound; the fitted floor matches the
one that was put in; and the fit is bit-identical between runs.

### Fitted against geometric, on the three links that have data

| Link | budget | M11e geometric | slice's own | fitted Δτ | fitted as length | floor | fit improvement | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` | 0.125 mm | 0.247 mm | **0.439 mm** | 0.00 ps | 0.000 mm | −17.7 dB | 0.0 dB | `under-floor` |
| `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` | 0.125 mm | 0.219 mm | 0.219 mm | 0.00 ps | 0.000 mm | −24.6 dB | 0.0 dB | `under-floor` |
| `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | 0.125 mm | 0.359 mm | 0.359 mm | **1.33 ps** | **0.198 mm** | −34.3 dB | 0.82 dB | `under-floor` |

Four things this says, and only the first is comfortable.

**The fit produces no false positives on this board.** Every link comes back
`under-floor`, which is the honest verdict for data whose floor sits where these do:
it says *no more than this*, and refuses to claim a measurement. M12's scalar, on
the same class of data, confidently flagged the wrong pairs. That is the
improvement, and it is a negative result rather than a positive one.

**Where it does find something, it agrees with the geometry to the right order.**
`REFCLK` is the only link whose fit finds any skew at all — 1.33 ps, which at its
own 6.2 ps/mm is 0.198 mm against a geometric 0.359 mm. Fifty-five percent of the
right answer, on a run that stopped with energy at −12 dB. It is the only one of the
three with real skew *and* the only one the fit reacts to, which is the correlation
one would want; with three points and one of them unconverged it is not evidence.

**The slicer is not always faithful, and now it says so.** `slice_skew_mm` was added
in M13c precisely so the comparison could be checked rather than assumed, and it
earned itself immediately: on `PCIE_TX` the *sliced* structure carries **0.439 mm**
of skew where the routed pair has 0.247 mm. That link is the AC-coupled one, where
the slicer shorts out two capacitors with a straight bridge per half, and the two
bridges are not the same length. A fitted delay is a measurement of what the solver
saw; on that link, what the solver saw is 78 % more out of length than the board is.
The other two agree to four decimal places.

**The thresholds are set from this, and the basis is thin.**
`SKEW_FIT_IMPROVEMENT_DB` and `SKEW_FIT_FLOOR_DB` are both 3.0. The measured floors
here span −17.7 to −34.3 dB — a 17 dB spread across three links of one board, the
same phenomenon M12 measured at 25 dB across eleven — and the fit improvements on
links with no resolvable skew are 0.0, 0.0 and 0.82 dB. A 3 dB bar sits nearly four
times the largest observed noise improvement, which is the right shape of margin and
is calibrated on three points rather than on eleven. **That is the number M13c's
acceptance asked to be set from the full board's measured noise, and it is not.**

### What this does not license

Promotion of a skew finding to an error stays out of scope, and now for a
*measured* reason rather than a precautionary one: the fit has been exercised on
three links, one of which was unconverged and one of which the slicer distorted.
Its false-positive rate on this evidence is zero out of three, which is not a rate.

## 4. Hygiene

### The container outlives nothing

`si/runner.py` cleaned up on the timeout path only. The M10–M12 chain paid for that
twice: a killed session left a sixteen-core FDTD run going eleven minutes later, and
a relaunch then had two containers writing one working directory. Three paths now:

* **A context manager** (`running_container`) reaps on any way out of the block — a
  return, an exception, a timeout, a `KeyboardInterrupt`.
* **Signal and `atexit` handlers** reap on interruption, chaining to whatever
  handler was there before so the exit status still says the process was signalled.
* **A pre-flight check** refuses to start a second solver against a directory
  something is already writing. This is the one that survives `SIGKILL`, which
  nothing can catch, and containers now carry an `aipcb.si.work` label so it can
  find an orphan whoever started it.

`tests/test_simulation.py::TestContainerLifetime` covers all three, and its last
test is the milestone's own: spawn a real container, `SIGTERM` the client
mid-solve, and assert nothing survives.

**It caught a real orphan during this milestone's own work**, which is the sort of
evidence that is better than a test. A fine-mesh experiment was `SIGKILL`ed by a
timeout; the next run refused to start with

```
aipcb-si-795765-85025756 is already solving in .../si-fine/PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP;
a second solver writing the same directory produces results that belong to neither.
```

which is exactly the situation that produced the chain's worst simulation hour.

### One pair, one skew finding, one number

the difference:

| where | code | number | what it actually was |
|---|---|---|---|
| `_match_lengths` | `diff-pair-skew` | 0.251 mm | the two halves' **tracks**, **before** the meander pass ran |

| | before | after | why |
|---|---|---|---|
| skew findings | 6 | 4 | **de-duplication took 6 findings on 2 pairs down to 2**; the geometry change then put two more pairs over budget, taking it back up to 4 |
| wall-hugging | 3 | 1 | the narrower pairs stand off their neighbours better |
| library mismatch | 1 | 1 | KiCad's, unremovable, unchanged since M11 |

Neither number would be visible if only the total were reported.

### The roadmap sweep

Marked done: the `_repair` cross-net defect, the coplanar impedance model, and the
container leak. Added: a coplanar model for inner layers, solving the pour standoff
from the impedance rather than the other way round, the simulation's new dependence

## 6. Open questions

* **`|Sdd21|` above unity on the transmit link, and only on that one.** At the
  refined mesh `PCIE_TXP/N` reads 1.04–1.22 across its whole band — a systematic
  excess rather than a spike — while `PCIE_RXP/N`, the same board at the same cell
  size, stays at 0.85–0.99 and is marked usable. Energy decayed to −63 dB on both,
  so it is not truncation. The transmit link is the one whose two coupling
  capacitors the slicer bridges into copper, which is the obvious suspect and is
  not evidence. The tool says `usable: false` and refuses to publish an impedance
  verdict for it, which is the right behaviour and is not an explanation.
* **The mode-conversion floor is still unexplained.** M13c fits it as a flat term
  and reports it; nothing says where it comes from. The experiment that would
  settle it is the one M12 named and neither milestone ran: route one pair twice on
  one board, once matched and once with a known injected skew, and difference the
  two curves. Differencing cancels the floor, which is the thing in the way.
* **The fit cannot tell intra-pair skew from anything else with the same frequency
  signature.** Any mechanism that converts differential to common mode as
  `sin(π f Δτ)` fits as skew, including a length difference introduced by the
  slicer itself. `slice_skew_mm` measures that one rather than assuming it away, and
  the measurement is not reassuring on every link: two of the three agree with the
  router to four decimal places and `PCIE_TXP/N` does not — 0.439 mm sliced against
  0.247 mm routed, because the two bridges the slicer lays across its coupling
  capacitors are not the same length. Making those bridges equal-length is a
  contained fix in `si/slice.py` and it was not made here.
* **Should the pour stand further off a controlled-impedance pair?** M12 asked it
  and M13b made it consequential: the pour gap is now an input to the width, and
  validation warns when it is tight enough that an etch tolerance on it matters.
  Nothing in aipcb has an opinion about what the right standoff *is*.
* **M12's "deliberately degraded case" is still not run**, and M13 did not run it
  either. It is the same experiment as the one above — one pair, twice, differing in
  one known thing — and it would answer the floor question and the
  does-simulation-discriminate question at once. Two milestones have now named it
  and neither has spent the hour.
* **The 100 Ω class now derives a 0.138 mm trace.** That is 5.4 mil, inside what a
  normal process etches but close to the edge of one, and it carries about 1.7× the
  resistance of the 0.239 mm trace it replaces. Nothing checks a derived width
  against a fabrication minimum, and after M13b the derivation can produce narrow
  ones from a perfectly ordinary-looking source.

M13 shipped its structural work and left its acceptance half-closed. This section

milestone asked for: free work first, so that the cheap answers could cancel the
expensive ones. Two of them did.

Everything below was measured on 2026-08-22 on the same machine as M13 (16 cores,

## 1. What `check` can and cannot see

### 1.1 The crossing tracks: the pipeline was not the problem

M13a's fix was confirmed by KiCad reporting 2 `tracks_crossing` before and 0 after,
and the obvious next question was why a board the chain had checked repeatedly had
been carrying two shorts. It had not been.

`PRSNT` is not in `examples/pcie-sata`'s netlist. The strap exists only in the
reproduction M11 built by hand and M13a turned into a test, so no board `check` ever
ran on had `PRSNT` copper on it, and none of them had a crossing. M13's own report
says as much in passing — *"all eleven examples come back with zero crossings,
before and after the fix — the defect was latent"* — and the latency is the whole
answer.

Measured rather than argued. A worktree at `ac4f0ce`, the commit before M13a, with
the strap added to the source and the *real* `aipcb check` run over it:

```
aipcb check, examples/pcie-sata + the A1-B17 strap, on the pre-M13a tree
  error    kicad-tracks-crossing            x2
  ...
  errors: 2      drc ran: True   counts: {'error': 2, 'warning': 1}
  raw kicad violations: {'tracks_crossing': 2, 'lib_footprint_mismatch': 1}
```

Two errors, correct category, correct severity, mapped back to the source like
everything else. **There was no gap here**, and `tests/test_check_loop.py::
TestNothingIsSilentlyFiltered::test_two_crossing_tracks_are_reported_as_an_error`
is what stops that answer from rotting: it lays two tracks of different nets across
each other on `routing-demo` and asserts an error comes back out of the real
`run_drc` with a hint attached.

### 1.2 The filter that is real, and is one level down

Asking the question properly turned up a different answer to it.

`kicad-cli pcb drc --severity-all` does not mean *all severities*. The report it
writes says what it included:

```
included_severities: ['error', 'warning', 'exclusion']
```

`ignore` is not in that list. A rule KiCad holds at `ignore` is dropped **inside
KiCad**, before any JSON reaches this project, and nothing in the report admits that
a category was suppressed — `included_severities` reads the same either way.

Proven by making a real violation disappear. Two crossing tracks on `routing-demo`,
then `tracks_crossing` pinned to `ignore` in the project file:

| | reported |
|---|---|
| as generated | `kicad-shorting-items` ×1, `kicad-solder-mask-bridge` ×3, **`kicad-tracks-crossing` ×1**, 5 errors |
| `tracks_crossing: ignore` | `kicad-shorting-items` ×1, `kicad-solder-mask-bridge` ×3, — , 4 errors |

Same `included_severities` both times. One error fewer, and no explanation anywhere.

**So which rules is KiCad 9.0.8 holding at `ignore`?** Nothing in `kicad-cli` will
say, and `pcbnew`'s Python bindings do not expose the severity map (checked). Two
independent readings instead:

* KiCad ships 37 project templates and demos that carry a full `rule_severities`
  map, giving a catalogue of **60 DRC rule names** and what KiCad writes for each.
  Four come out `ignore`: `footprint_filters_mismatch` (37 of 37),
  `missing_courtyard` (29), `npth_inside_courtyard` (26), `pth_inside_courtyard`
  (25).
* Then measured directly, by building a board that violates the rule and running
  DRC with and without the pin:

| rule | violations built | reported by default | reported when pinned |
|---|---|---|---|
| `missing_courtyard` | 6 footprints stripped of their courtyard | **0** | 6 |
| `pth_inside_courtyard` | 14 through-holes inside a courtyard | **0** | 14 |

`missing_courtyard` is the one that matters. `courtyards_overlap` is how a placement
is checked for legality, and a footprint with no courtyard opts out of it silently —
so the rule that would have told you the check is not running is itself not running.

**The fix.** Every generated `.kicad_pro` now pins
`aipcb.compile.project.DRC_SEVERITIES`: those four rules at `warning`, unconditional,
whether or not the design needs any other rule changed. It is `warning` and not
`error` because all four are advisory about how a board was drawn.

**What it changed on the corpus: one finding, on the flagship board.** All eleven
examples were re-run with the four rules pinned:

| example | newly visible |
|---|---|
| ten of the eleven | nothing |
| `pcie-sata` | `footprint_filters_mismatch` ×1, warning, on `U1` |

And it is a true positive. `U1`'s *symbol* is a connector stand-in — KiCad ships none
for a PCIe-to-SATA controller, which the example's own header says — so its footprint
filters read `Connector*:*_1x??_*` while the part declares
`Package_DFN_QFN:QFN-48-1EP_7x7mm_P0.5mm_EP5.15x5.15mm`. KiCad has been able to say
"that symbol was not meant for that footprint" the whole time and was holding the
rule at `ignore`. It is now in `KNOWN_ISSUES` with that explanation rather than
suppressed.

The first pass at this measurement said "nothing, anywhere", and it was wrong: it
counted `payload["violations"]` and a schematic-parity finding arrives in
`payload["schematic_parity"]`. The suite caught it — `test_examples_check_clean`
failed on `pcie-sata` — which is the second time in two milestones that the tests
have caught the report being wrong about its own measurement.

The rule names are written down in the test suite as `KICAD_DRC_RULES` with the
version and the date attached, per ADR 0009's discipline, because they are KiCad's
and they will move.

### 1.3 Two other things the enumeration turned up

**A hint that had never once fired.** `_HINTS` was keyed on `courtyard_overlap`.
KiCad's rule is `courtyards_overlap`, plural. From M4 to M13.5 the hint for the most
common placement violation was attached to a rule name that does not exist. Fixed,
and `test_every_hint_names_a_rule_kicad_actually_has` now checks every key against
the catalogue.

**Copper outside the board outline is invisible to DRC.** Measured on 9.0.8: the
same two crossing tracks, moved outside `Edge.Cuts`, produce no `tracks_crossing`,
no `clearance` and no `copper_edge_clearance` — only `track_dangling`, which is
about their ends rather than about where they are. Nothing in aipcb puts copper
there and this is not a bug report; it is a test
(`test_copper_outside_the_outline_is_invisible_to_drc`) so that a change which
starts to would not be verified by a DRC that cannot see it.

### 1.4 The one that was actually broken: `check` was not a function of the source

The step-3 measurement below wanted the pour gap along each pair, so it ran `check`
into a directory and read the filled board. The first attempt crashed after the
board was written; the second ran into the same directory and produced a board with
three long straight tracks on it that no router had drawn.

That is not a measurement artefact. Checking the same unchanged design repeatedly
into one `--out` directory:

| run | copper | routed | DRC errors |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1108.4 mm | 90 / 90 | 0 |
| 2 | **1852.7 mm** | **68 / 90** | **1** (`clearance`) |
| 3 | **2462.3 mm** | **59 / 90** | **1** |

`check` routes the board it builds and writes the copper into it. The next build
read that copper back, and `compile/preserve.py` says in its own header that
`segment`, `via` and `zone` items are *"never generated, and so always belong to a
human"* — so it kept them, and the router then routed the design again on top of
them.

**That sentence was true when it was written and stopped being true twice**: at M7,
when routing started writing segments and vias, and at M10, when the source started
declaring zones. Nothing noticed, because nothing had ever checked the same design
twice into one place. It is the same failure mode this repository's `CLAUDE.md`
warns about for external tools, arriving from inside.

The fix is one argument: `check` builds with `fresh=True`. A check is a question
about the source, so it has to be a function of the source alone. Preserving a
human's edits across a rebuild is still `aipcb build`'s job and is untouched.
`TestCheckIsAFunctionOfTheSource` asserts the board, the routing summary and the DRC
counts are identical across two consecutive checks; before the fix it fails on the
first assertion.

## 2. The skew budgets, against the standards themselves

M13 turned two over-budget pairs into four and the milestone asked whether the
budgets were the problem. They are not, and the answer took no simulation at all.

### PCIe

**PCI Express Card Electromechanical Specification, Revision 3.0 (2013-07-21),
§4.7.7, "Skew within the Differential Pair":**

> The differential pair shall be routed such that the skew within differential
> pairs is less than 0.127 mm (5 mils) for the add-in card and 0.254 mm (10 mils)
> for the system board.

`examples/pcie-sata` is an add-in card — it has PCIe edge fingers and lives in a
slot — so **0.127 mm** is the number. The class carried 0.125 mm, which is the
standard's figure rounded down with nothing recording what it was rounded from. The
source now says 0.127 with §4.7.7 quoted beside it. Checked before making the
change: routing is byte-identical at both values (same SHA-256 over the whole
board), so this moved no copper and did not invalidate the simulation batch that was
running at the time.

**So the four pairs are genuinely over the standard**, by 1.5× to 2.8×:

| pair | as built | PCIe CEM add-in-card limit | over by |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` | 0.191 mm | 0.127 mm | 1.50× |
| `PCIE_TXN_C+PCIE_TXP_C` | 0.247 mm | 0.127 mm | 1.95× |
| `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` | 0.219 mm | 0.127 mm | 1.72× |
| `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | 0.359 mm | (see below) | — |

**This is not a specification fix, and one argument has to be withdrawn.** M11 and
M12 both reached for "1.2–2.2 ps against a 125 ps unit interval is absorbable", and
M13 repeated it. §4.7.7's stated rationale is common-mode conversion and therefore
**EMI**, not timing: 0.127 mm is about 0.8 ps in this laminate, so the standard is
already asking for something far tighter than a timing argument would produce, and
answering it with a timing argument answers a question it is not asking. The four
pairs need geometric work — placement, so the two halves break out symmetrically —
and that is on the roadmap with these measurements attached, not done here.

**REFCLK is not covered by §4.7.7 at all**, and the check turned up a second thing.
§4.7's budgets are the *Link's* — the data lanes. The reference clock is routed
"according to best-known clock routing rules", and §2.1.1 says its timings assume
**nominal 100 Ω** differential. `examples/pcie-sata` carries REFCLK in the `pcie_rx`
class at **85 Ω**. That is a real mismatch between the board and the standard, it is
not a skew question, and fixing it means giving REFCLK its own net class — a
geometry change M13.5 had no mandate to make. On the roadmap, and §4 below reads
REFCLK's simulated impedance in that light.

### SATA

**Serial ATA Revision 3.5 Gold (2020-07-01) gives no intra-pair skew figure for a
PCB trace**, which is the first useful thing to know: the number usually quoted as
"the SATA skew spec" is one of three other things.

| what | where | limit |
|---|---|---|
| `tskewTx` — the transmitter's own differential skew, at the mated connector | Table 54, Gen1i/Gen2i/Gen3i | **20 ps max** |
| `tskewRx` — what a receiver must tolerate | Table 57, Gen3i | 30 ps |
| mated cable assembly intra-pair skew | §6.12 | ≤ 10 ps |

A board trace between silicon and connector spends out of the first. The class
budget of 0.25 mm is about **1.6 ps** in this laminate — under a twelfth of the
transmitter's entire budget. So the budget is conservative by design rather than
wrong, it is **confirmed rather than loosened**, and the eight SATA pairs meet it
with room to spare at 0.000–0.041 mm as built. The class comment now carries the
table numbers instead of the rule of thumb it used to cite.

### One more thing the standard says, which changes how §3 reads

**PCIe CEM r3.0 §4.7.8:** an 8.0 GT/s data pair's PCB differential impedance "must
be in the range of **70 Ω to 100 Ω**". The project's own ±10 % band on an 85 Ω
target is 76.5–93.5 Ω — *tighter than the standard*. `PCIE_RX` at 72.8 Ω misses the
project's band and sits inside the standard's. That does not make the residual
uninteresting, and §3 does not treat it as closed; it does mean the board this
milestone is arguing about would pass the requirement it was designed against.

## 3. `PCIE_RX` at −14.4 %: one hypothesis, refuted, and what the residual actually is

The milestone asked for **one** hypothesis, tested with the minimum simulation.
Two were available for free and both were answered without starting a solver.

### The pour gap is not the cause

The coplanar model is handed `clearance_mm` — 0.15 mm — as the gap to the pour. What
the built board has, measured off the filled copper by sampling each pair's
centre-line every 0.1 mm and taking edge-to-edge distance to the `GND` pour on the
same layer:

| pair | layer | as-built width | pour gap: min | median |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_TXP/N` | F.Cu | 0.1846 mm | 0.1505 mm | 0.1505 mm |
| `PCIE_RXP/N` | B.Cu | 0.1846 mm | 0.1505 mm | 0.1505 mm |
| `PCIE_RXP/N` | F.Cu | 0.1846 mm | 0.1505 mm | 0.1518–0.1558 mm |
| `REFCLKP/N` | B.Cu | 0.1846 mm | 0.1505 mm | 0.1505 mm |
| all eight `SATA*` | F.Cu | 0.1379 mm | 0.1505 mm | 0.1505 mm |

**0.1505 mm against a modelled 0.150 mm, on every pair and every layer** — 0.3 %
out, in the direction that would make the impedance *higher* rather than lower. The
widths are the derived ones to four decimal places. The model's inputs are the
board's geometry, and the hypothesis is refuted.

A second free check went with it: `pcie_tx` sits on F.Cu over In1 and `pcie_rx` on
B.Cu over In2, and if those two dielectrics differed the two classes could not
correctly derive the same width. The stackup is symmetric — `prepreg_top` and
`prepreg_bottom` are both 0.2104 mm of 7628 — so that is not it either.

### What the two failing links do share

Read off the solver inputs rather than argued:

| link | port layers | reference planes | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` | 0, 0, 0, 0 | 1, 1, 1, 1 | 80.0 Ω, **−5.8 %** |
| `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` | **3, 0, 3, 0** | **2, 1, 2, 1** | 72.8 Ω, −14.4 % |
| `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | **0, 3, 0, 3** | **1, 2, 1, 2** | 49.8 Ω, −41.4 % (unconverged) |
| `SATA*` × 8 | 0, 0, 0, 0 | 1, 1, 1, 1 | §5 |

**The two links whose two ends are on different layers are exactly the two that miss
the band, and the ones that stay on one layer do not.** Two out of two, and nought
out of one among the PCIe links that keep both ends on F.Cu.

That matters because of what the estimator is.
`si/results.py::analyse` reads a **median input impedance** over a band trimmed to a
whole number of standing-wave periods. That recovers the characteristic impedance of
a *uniform* line from the ripple its own length puts on the input impedance. A link
that changes layer half way along is not a uniform line: it is roughly 2.6 mm on
F.Cu over In1, a via barrel, and 16.8 mm on B.Cu over In2 — a cascade of two
sections referenced to two different planes. The median of what port 1 sees through
that is not either section's Z₀, and calling it one is a category error rather than
an inaccuracy.

The slicer already knew — its own note on both slices says *"the pair changes layer,
so its two ends are referenced to different planes; the impedance reported is the
whole link, not either half"* — but the number went out to the report and the ±10 %
verdict anyway, and M13's table presented it as a trace impedance beside `PCIE_TX`'s.

### The measurement that would have settled it, and why it cannot

The S-parameters are already on disk, so a TDR — the step response of Γ_dd,
inverse-transformed — costs nothing and separates a cascade into its sections. It
was run. It cannot answer this question:

* the sweep stops at 8 GHz, so the step's rise time is ~63 ps, which is **4.5–5 mm**
  of spatial resolution in this laminate;
* the F.Cu section is **2.6 mm** long.

The section under suspicion is half the size of the smallest thing the data can
resolve. What the profiles do show is that neither link has any sustained region at
72.8 Ω — both sit near 87–88 Ω at the port and swing above it — which is consistent
with two ~85 Ω sections and a discontinuity, and is not evidence enough to say so.

### Outcome: characterised, open, and no longer mislabelled

The residual is **not closed**. What changed is that it is no longer reported as a
trace impedance:

* `si/slice.py` gains `Slice.spans_layers` and `Slice.spans_planes`, both in
  `slice.json`;
* `Metrics.spans_layers` carries it into `result.json`;
* and a link whose ends are on different layers now emits, in its own output, *"the
  two ends of this pair are on different layers, so the span measured here contains
  a via transition and a change of reference plane; the impedance below is the whole
  link's and is not the characteristic impedance of either section"*.

The number is still published, because it is a real measurement of the whole link.
What stops is offering it as something to compare against a fabricator's coupon.

Verified end to end by re-running `aipcb simulate` over M13's own three PCIe
directories. Every slice digest still matches, so all three are cache hits and the
whole thing costs no solver time at all:

```
PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP   spans_layers=True    budget=0.127   Z=72.78
    -> the two ends of this pair are on different layers, so the span measured
       here contains a via transition and a change of reference plane; ...
PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP   spans_layers=False   budget=0.127   Z=80.05
REFCLKN+REFCLKP     spans_layers=True    budget=0.127   Z=49.78
    -> ...
```

`result.json` is now rewritten on a cache hit as well as on a fresh run. The metrics
are recomputed every time — they are derived from the S-parameters, not from the
run — so a stored copy that was written by older analysis code was quietly
disagreeing with what the tool would say today. Every cached result on this machine
was still claiming there was nothing unusual about a link that changes layer. The
`digest` and the `run` block are the run and do not move.

**What would actually close it** is a slicer that can cut one side of a transition
and port it on its own — which is the thing M13 said the slicer does not do, still
does not do, and is now on the roadmap with the reason written next to it. The
cheaper half-step, and the one worth doing first, is to widen the sweep: at 20 GHz
the TDR resolution would be under 2 mm and this would be a five-minute question.

## 4. `REFCLK`: blocked, with a diagnosis, and M13's hypothesis refuted

**The iteration budget was set at eight reduced-settings runs before any were
started**, per the milestone. Five were used. What they bought is a diagnosis rather
than a converged number, so REFCLK's simulation is recorded as
**blocked-with-diagnosis** and the −41.4 % in M13's table stays what it was: a number
read off a run with 6 % of its energy still in the box.

### What "does not converge" actually looks like, from data M13 already had

Nobody had plotted it. The solver logs on disk carry an energy sample every few
hundred timesteps, and the shape settles the question before any new run:

| | `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` (converges) | `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` (does not) |
|---|---|---|
| peak energy | 9.25e-16 at ts 11 661 | 1.45e-15 at ts 15 488 |
| then | −8.8, −24.3, −26.7, −36.2, −41.0, −47.6, −52.8, −58.6, **−64.3 dB** | −6.1, −8.8, −9.2, −9.0, −8.8, −9.2, −9.1, −9.4, −8.6, −10.2, −9.1, **−10.2 dB** |
| shape | monotonic exponential decay over ~40 000 steps | **falls 9 dB, then flat for 42 000 steps** |

REFCLK does not decay slowly. It decays for about 7 000 timesteps, loses roughly 90 %
of its peak energy — the part that leaves through the ports — and then **stops**. The
remaining tenth sits in the box, oscillating between −8.3 and −10.8 dB, for the
entire rest of the run.

**This refutes M13's stated hypothesis.** M13 wrote: *"the step limit, not the model.
It is testable — raise `max_steps` for that class and watch the energy floor."* It is
not the step limit. Forty thousand more steps of a flat line is a flat line, and the
evidence for that was already in the log file M13 wrote. Raising `max_steps` would
have cost about eight minutes a run and produced the same −10 dB.

What it is, is a **trapped mode**: about a tenth of the injected energy is in a
resonance with no path to a port and nothing to dissipate it. FDTD in a lossless
structure will hold that forever.

### The iterations, and the rule that made them cheap

Reduced-settings iteration was mandatory, and two of the three levers the milestone
named turned out not to exist:

* **A coarser mesh is not available.** `si/inputs.py::grid_optimal_um` is defined as
  *never coarser than the setting*, so raising `grid_optimal_um` in the source does
  nothing — the geometry's own demand wins. Setting it to 60 µm left the cell at
  30.7 µm and, because `max_steps` scales with the ratio, quietly multiplied the step
  limit by 1.95 instead. That is correct behaviour and it means the mesh cannot be
  used as a reduced setting from the design file.
* **A narrower band breaks the measurement rather than cheapening it.** Sweeping to
  4 GHz produced `"the swept band is shorter than one standing-wave period on this
  pair"` and a group delay of **−35 ps** over 19.7 mm of conductor — the tool's own
  "the ports are not measuring the trace" note, correctly fired.

What does work is the step limit, applied to an already-generated slice. Every run
below re-exports the same slice with one thing changed and `max_steps` cut, so the
mesh, the band, the ports and the stackup are byte-identical to M13's own run.

| # | what changed | steps | energy at ts ≈ 20 000 | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| i1 | `grid_optimal_um` 60, band 0.1–4 GHz | 15 635 | — | **the levers do not work**; band too narrow to measure |
| i2 | *nothing* — control at reduced steps | 20 000 | −6.96 dB | reproduces M13's trace **sample for sample** |
| i3 | all 17 foreign-net items deleted | 20 000 | −5.87 dB | no better than the control |
| i4 | the same, run long enough to see the plateau | 48 000 | **−10.11 dB at ts 47 916** | **plateaus exactly as the control does** (−10.22 dB at ts 64 372) |
| i5 | `In2.Cu` tied to `GND`, foreign copper kept | 48 000 | see below | the last suspect |

i2 is worth its two and a half minutes on its own: its energy trace is
value-for-value identical to the first 20 000 steps of M13's full run, which is what
makes the reduced runs admissible as evidence at all.

### What has been ruled out, and at what cost

**Foreign floating copper in the slice: not the trap.** REFCLK's slice carries the
whole transmit quartet — `PCIE_TXN`, `PCIE_TXP`, `PCIE_TXN_C`, `PCIE_TXP_C`, twelve
segments — with the AC-coupling capacitors *not* bridged, because the slicer bridges
only the pair under test. Each is an open-ended conductor over ground with no port
and no loss, which is a textbook half-wave resonator in the middle of the swept band,
and it was the obvious suspect. Deleting all seventeen foreign items changes the
energy trace by under 1 dB and does not change the plateau. Two runs, about five
minutes.

**Boundary proximity: not the trap, and free.** All three PCIe slices put their pair
exactly **1.592 mm** from the outline — it is the launch margin, uniform by
construction — so the link that converges and the link that does not are equally far
from the wall. No simulation needed.

**The step limit: refuted before spending anything**, from the trace above.

### What is left, and what M13.5 did with it

The structural difference between `REFCLKP/N` and `PCIE_RXP/N` — same class, same
width, same gap, same layers, one converges and one does not — is what remains:

| | `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` |
|---|---|---|
| slice | 5.260 × 25.297 mm | 4.435 × 25.297 mm |
| `GND` vias inside the slice | **5** | **7** |
| planes | `GND` on F.Cu, B.Cu, In1.Cu · `P3V3` on In2.Cu | the same |
| final energy | **−10.2 dB** | −49.9 / −61.3 dB |

`In2.Cu` carries `P3V3` and, inside the slice, is connected to nothing at all: a
floating plate between two grounded ones, with the pair's own via barrel passing
through its antipad. That is the classic way to excite a parallel-plate mode, and
the only thing that damps it is stitching — of which REFCLK's window has five vias
and RX's has seven.

### i5: the floating plane holds some of it, and not all of it

`In2.Cu`'s zone was renamed from `P3V3` to `GND` in the slice — one net, nothing else
touched, so the plate stops floating and joins the two that are already grounded.

| | control (i2/i4 and M13's run) | i5, `In2.Cu` tied to `GND` |
|---|---|---|
| ts 20 000 | −7.48 dB | −8.66 dB |
| ts 24 000 | −8.88 dB | **−14.11 dB** |
| ts 32 000 | −8.34 dB | −14.18 dB |
| ts 45 000 | −9.98 dB | −15.07 dB |
| final | **−10.2 dB** | **−14.1 / −15.6 dB** |

**It is worth about 4–5 dB, and it is still a plateau.** The floating plane holds a
share of the trapped energy — roughly two thirds of what was stuck comes out once it
is grounded — and something else holds the rest. Convergence needs about −40 dB;
this is −15.

So the honest position, at five of eight budgeted iterations:

> **`REFCLKN+REFCLKP` is blocked, with a diagnosis.** The −41.4 % in M13's table is
> read off a run with 6 % of its energy still in the box and remains unusable. The
> failure is a trapped resonance, not an insufficient step limit — which is the
> opposite of what M13 recorded. Of the four suspects the milestone named, three are
> refuted by measurement (step limit, foreign copper in the slice, boundary
> proximity) and the fourth — a resonant structure inside the slice — is confirmed as
> *present* and only partly located: the un-grounded `In2.Cu` plane accounts for
> 4–5 dB of it.

Stopping here rather than spending the remaining three iterations is deliberate. The
next experiment is a board question rather than a solver one — REFCLK's slice has
**five** `GND` stitching vias where `PCIE_RXP/N`'s has **seven**, for the same
transition depth through the same stack — and answering it properly means changing
copper, which this milestone was told not to do. It is on the roadmap with this
measurement attached.

### One observation that is not evidence, and is worth writing down

M12 simulated the same pair at a 50 µm mesh on a wider trace and its energy reached
**−41.2 dB**. M13's run reaches −10.2. Three things changed between them — the mesh
(50 → 30.7 µm), the trace (0.289 → 0.185 mm) and the routing (44 → 42 vias) — so
this attributes to nothing. It is recorded because it means the trapped mode is not
a permanent feature of this geometry: there is a configuration of this same pair, on
this same board, that does converge, and M12 has it.

## 5. The eight SATA links, and the cost that made them impossible

M13 deferred the eight SATA links and left a batch running for them. **That batch
could not have succeeded**, and finding out why is the useful part of this section.

### The batch M13 left running failed, twice, at exactly 1800.0 s

It was still going when this session opened, an hour and fifty minutes in, with
nothing to show:

```
simulating pcie-sata into .../final
  SATA0_RXN+SATA0_RXP      failed  1800.0 s
  SATA0_TXN+SATA0_TXP      failed  1800.0 s
```

1800 s is `si/runner.py::DEFAULT_TIMEOUT_S`. Every SATA link was going to hit it.

**Why the estimate was wrong.** M13 measured the slice — 3.63 M cells at 22.9 µm,
`max_steps` 87 336 — and projected 9–13 minutes a link from the solver's throughput.
Two things the projection missed:

* **a pair is two solver runs, not one.** gerber2ems excites each half of the pair
  in turn, so the 87 336 steps are paid twice;
* **throughput is not 800–1200 MC/s on a slice this size.** Measured here on a
  SATA slice: **335 MC/s with the machine to itself**, 237 MC/s while anything else
  was running. In the units that matter it is **79 timesteps a second**.

So a SATA pair is 174 672 timesteps at 79 steps a second — **about 37 minutes on an
idle sixteen-core machine**, against a per-pair budget of 30. The projection was out
by a factor of three to four, and the failure mode was not "slow" but "reported
nothing at all": a timeout produces no `result.json`, so the next run re-slices and
starts again from zero.

The arithmetic that matters for anyone planning the next batch: **eight SATA links
is about five hours**, and it is five hours whether or not the timeout is set high
enough to let them finish.

Nothing warns about this before a batch starts, and everything needed to warn is
known at slice time: the cell count, the step limit and the machine's own measured
throughput are all in hand before the container is launched. On the roadmap.

### Where the eleven links stand

The batch was relaunched with `--timeout 7200` on the tree as committed. The geometry
is byte-identical to M13's — the only source change is §2's skew budget, and that was
checked to move no copper *before* it was made — so every slice digest still matches
and the three PCIe links come back as cache hits at no solver cost at all.

**One SATA link was re-simulated before the machine was needed for the suite, and
it came back unusable.** That is the more useful half of this section:

```
SATA0_RXN+SATA0_RXP   3 216 s   Zdiff 165.15 ohm against a 100 ohm target (+65.1 %)
                                energy -33.9 dB, |Sdd21| = 1.33, usable: false
```

(3 216 s and not the 37 minutes projected above because the test suite was running
beside it for most of that; the 79 timesteps a second the projection uses was
measured with the machine to itself. Either way it is past 1800 s.)

`|Sdd21|` above unity is more energy out than went in, so the extraction is not
physical and the tool refuses to publish an impedance verdict for it — correctly.
Two things about it are worth stating plainly.

**It is off in the opposite direction from every M12 number.** M12's SATA links read
62.5–91.9 Ω against 100 — *below* target, which is what the bare-microstrip model
error predicts. This reads 165 Ω. That is not a model error in either direction; it
is an extraction that has stopped meaning anything.

**And it is now three links out of four.** On the M13b geometry, of everything
simulated to date:

| link | `usable` | why not |
|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` | **true** | — |
| `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` | false | `\|Sdd21\|` 1.23 |
| `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | published as `warn` | `\|Sdd21\|` 1.13, energy −12 dB |
| `SATA0_RXN+SATA0_RXP` | false | `\|Sdd21\|` 1.33, energy −34 dB |

M13 recorded the transmit link's `|Sdd21| > 1` as a single odd result with the
AC-coupling bridges as its suspect. **It is not a single odd result.** Three of the
four links measured on this geometry fail the same physical sanity check, and the
one that passes is the one whose energy decayed furthest (−61 dB). The common thread
is incomplete decay, not coupling capacitors — which puts it in the same family as
§4 rather than in the family M13 filed it under, and makes "simulate the other seven
SATA links" the wrong next move. **The next move is to find out why energy stopped
leaving these slices after M13b changed the mesh and the trace width**, because
until that is answered a bigger batch buys more unusable numbers at 37 minutes each.

This is on the roadmap, and it is the single most consequential thing M13.5 found in
§§4–5.

**Coverage is not eleven, and saying otherwise would be the reframing this project
exists not to do.** What is measured, and what is not:

| Link | Target | M12 | M13 | M13.5 | spans layers? | inside ±10 %? |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` | 85 Ω | 66.6 Ω (−21.6 %) | **80.0 Ω (−5.8 %)** | unchanged, cached | no | **yes**, but `usable: false` (\|Sdd21\| 1.23) |
| `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` | 85 Ω | 61.5 Ω (−27.6 %) | **72.8 Ω (−14.4 %)** | unchanged, cached; now labelled | **yes** | no — and §3 says what the number is |
| `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | 85 Ω | 50.9 Ω (−40.1 %) | 49.8 Ω (−41.4 %) | **blocked-with-diagnosis**, §4 | **yes** | no, and the number is not a measurement |
| `SATA0_RXN+SATA0_RXP` | 100 Ω | 91.9 Ω (−8.1 %) | not run | **165.2 Ω, `usable: false`** | no | **no verdict** — the extraction is not physical |
| `SATA*` × 7 | 100 Ω | 62.5–91.9 Ω | not run | **not run** | no | — |

**So the acceptance clause "all 11 links" is not met, at 4 of 11 simulated and 1 of
those 4 usable.** The seven that did not run are about **four and a half hours** of
solver time at the measured rate, and on the evidence of the one that did, that time
would mostly buy `usable: false`.

### The fitted-vs-geometric table, at the coverage it actually has

M13c's acceptance asked for this on all eleven links and got three. M13.5 does not
change that number, so the table below is M13's three with the corrected budget —
and the honest headline is that **the fit still has three points and one of them is
unconverged, so its false-positive rate is still nought out of three, which is still
not a rate.**

| Link | budget | geometric | slice's own | fitted Δτ | fitted as length | floor | fit improvement | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PCIE_TXN+PCIE_TXP` | 0.127 mm | 0.247 mm | **0.439 mm** | 0.00 ps | 0.000 mm | −17.7 dB | 0.0 dB | `under-floor` |
| `PCIE_RXN+PCIE_RXP` | 0.127 mm | 0.219 mm | 0.219 mm | 0.00 ps | 0.000 mm | −24.6 dB | 0.0 dB | `under-floor` |
| `REFCLKN+REFCLKP` | 0.127 mm | 0.359 mm | 0.359 mm | **1.33 ps** | **0.198 mm** | −34.3 dB | 0.82 dB | `under-floor` |
| the eight `SATA*` | 0.25 mm | 0.000–0.041 mm | — | — | — | — | — | not simulated |

Two things M13.5 adds to it rather than to the numbers.

**The budget column is now the standard's**, not a rounded version of it (§2), and it
moves no verdict: 0.219 mm was over 0.125 and is over 0.127.

**The geometric column is the one worth trusting on the four PCIe pairs**, and §2 is
why. The fit says `under-floor` on all three, which is the correct refusal for data
whose floor sits at −17 to −34 dB; the router's own measurement says 0.191–0.359 mm
against a standard that asks for 0.127. The verification layer that can currently
answer the question is the geometric one, and the frequency-domain one is not yet
able to contradict it — which is a thinner claim than "they agree", and is what the
evidence supports.

### Simulation time spent, per step, against M12's baseline

M12's eleven links cost **7 837 s of solver time — 2 h 11 min**. M13.5's spend, by
the milestone's own work order:

| step | what it needed | solver time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — check visibility | building boards and running `kicad-cli` | **0 s** |
| 2 — skew budgets | reading two published standards | **0 s** |
| 3 — the `PCIE_RX` residual | measuring the built board, and a TDR over S-parameters already on disk | **0 s** |
| 4 — `REFCLK` convergence | five reduced-settings runs: 319 s, 150 s, 137 s, 1 178 s, 957 s | **2 741 s — 46 min** |
| 5 — the SATA links | one link re-simulated; three cached PCIe links re-analysed at no cost | **3 216 s — 54 min** |

**Three of the five steps cost nothing**, which was the point of the ordering the
milestone imposed: the pour-gap hypothesis, the boundary-proximity suspect and M13's
own step-limit hypothesis were all settled by measuring something that already
existed. The one step that cost is the one that had to.

The reduced-settings rule is why step 4 is 46 minutes and not several hours. M13's
one full REFCLK run is 474 s; the whole diagnostic campaign is 2 741 s, and two of
its five runs — the two that refuted M13's own hypothesis and the foreign-copper
one — cost **150 s and 137 s** between them, because they ran 20 000 steps on a
slice that had already been generated. Iterating at full cost would have been about
eight minutes a hypothesis at best and, for the two long runs, forty.

The i1 line is worth reading as a cost as well as a result: 319 s spent discovering
that two of the three reduced-settings levers the milestone named do not exist on
this tool. That is the cheapest way to find it out and it is written down in §4 so
nobody spends it again.

## 7. What M13.5 leaves open

* **`REFCLK`'s trapped mode is located to within 4–5 dB, and not fully.** The
  floating `In2.Cu` plane holds about two thirds of what is stuck — grounding it in
  the slice moves the plateau from −10 dB to −15 dB — and something else holds the
  rest. The next experiment is a board question rather than a solver one: REFCLK's
  slice carries **five** `GND` stitching vias around its transition where
  `PCIE_RXP/N`, which converges to −50 dB through the same stack, carries **seven**.
  Testing it means changing copper, which is why M13.5 stopped.
* **The impedance estimator assumes a uniform line and nothing enforced it.** M13.5
  made a layer-spanning link *say* its number is the whole link's. It did not make
  the tool able to measure either section, and it did not stop the ±10 % verdict
  being taken against a number the estimator does not apply to. Widening the sweep to
  20 GHz would let a TDR separate them from data the existing pipeline already
  produces; that is the cheap half and it was not done.
* **Energy is not leaving these slices, and it is not one link's problem.** Three
  of the four links measured on the M13b geometry report more energy out than went
  in. §4 shows one of them trapping a tenth of its energy in a resonance; §5 shows
  the pattern is general. Nothing in M13.5 explains why M13b's change of mesh and
  trace width should have done this, and it is the question that gates every
  remaining simulation on this board.
* **`aipcb simulate` has no cost model.** M13 projected 9–13 minutes a SATA link and
  the real figure exceeds the tool's own 1800 s default timeout. The slice knows its
  cell count and its step limit before the container starts, so it could say so.
* **The four over-budget PCIe pairs still need geometry.** M13.5 confirmed the budget
  against the standard rather than moving it, which converts an open question into a
  scoped piece of work and does not do the work.
* **`preserve.py`'s premise is fixed where it bit and not where it lives.** `check`
  no longer merges, so the corruption is gone; the module still says `segment`, `via`
  and `zone` are "never generated" and `aipcb build` still believes it. A build over
  a routed board will preserve aipcb's own copper as though a human drew it. Nothing
  in the corpus does that today, and it is the next place this premise will bite.
* **M12's "deliberately degraded case" is still not run.** Three milestones have now
  named it. It would settle the mode-conversion floor and the
  does-simulation-discriminate question at once, and it is still an hour nobody has
  spent.
