# M10 — copper pours, stitching vias & plane integrity: delivery report

> ## ⛔ STOPPED — not delivered. A decision is needed before M10 can be built.
>
> M10's own guardrail says:
>
> > *If KiCad's CLI cannot fill zones headlessly in the installed version, stop and
> > present options before working around it.*
>
> **It cannot.** `kicad-cli` 9.0.8 has no zone-fill command, no fill flag, and no
> jobset job that fills; `pcb drc` does not fill zones before checking, and
> `pcb export gerbers` plots whatever fill data is already in the file and silently
> exports *no copper at all* for an unfilled pour. All four claims are measured
> below, positively rather than inferred from `--help`.
>
> KiCad's fill engine **is** reachable headlessly — via `pcbnew.ZONE_FILLER`, from
> the same 9.0.8 package — and it works, fast and byte-deterministically. But
> [ADR 0001](../decisions/0001-kicad-io.md) excluded `pcbnew` *up front, by the
> project brief*, and `kicad/cli.py` still states the exclusion as a design
> property. Reversing a brief-level exclusion, and with it the CI requirement for
> everyone, is the owner's call and not a subagent's.
>
> **Nothing was implemented. No schema, examples, tests or source files were
> touched.** This run delivered the two empirical investigations M10 asked for
> *before* writing code, plus the performance baseline the report requires. Both
> are recorded so that whoever resumes M10 does not repeat them.
>
> **What is needed: pick an option in [ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-pours.md)**
> ("The options"). The recommendation there is Option 1 — drive `pcbnew` in a
> subprocess, recorded as a deliberate narrowing of ADR 0001 — but the decision is
> deliberately left open.

## The measurements

Full detail, method and probe design in [ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-pours.md).
KiCad 9.0.8 (`kicad 9.0.8+dfsg-1`), measured 2026-08-21.

### 1. The `kicad-cli` fill surface: there isn't one

`kicad-cli` has six commands (`fp`, `jobset`, `pcb`, `sch`, `sym`, `version`);
`pcb` has exactly three (`drc`, `export`, `render`). No fill, no refill, no flag.
The jobset runner adds nothing — the job types compiled into 9.0.8 are `pcb_drc`,
`pcb_render`, `pcb_netlist` and eleven `pcb_export_*`.

Tested positively with a board carrying two `GND` vias 24 mm apart, no track, and
one unfilled `GND` zone spanning both. If a stage fills, the unconnected count
falls from 1 to 0:

| Stage | Unconnected | Filled? |
|---|---|---|
| `kicad-cli pcb drc --severity-all`, unfilled zone | **1** | no |
| the same, with the zone's `(fill yes …)` flag set | **1** | no |
| `kicad-cli pcb drc` after `ZONE_FILLER` | **0** | yes |

Gerbers answer identically: plotting `F.Cu` gives **596 bytes / 2 coordinate
records** unfilled versus **1066 bytes / 20** filled. An unfilled pour exports as
nothing, without a warning.

### 2. Fill determinism: byte-identical, on every run measured

The stability policy in M10b depends on this, and M10 says to measure it. Filling
the *same* input repeatedly, in *separate processes*:

| Board | Runs | Distinct output hashes |
|---|---|---|
| synthetic probe (1 zone, 2 vias) | 5 | **1** |
| `examples/usb-port` (2 zones, 31 pads, 92 segments, 4 vias) | 3 | **1** |

Byte-identical across the whole file, not merely the fill polygons. Eight runs on
two boards is evidence, not proof — thermal spokes and island removal are where
tie-breaking would plausibly leak — but nothing suggests nondeterminism, and M10b's
proposed policy (guarantee covers *unfilled build output*; filled output stable in
practice) is consistent with what was measured.

### 3. The milestone is achievable once unblocked

Not a promise — a measurement. `examples/usb-port`, built and routed by today's
code, with `GND` poured on `F.Cu` and `B.Cu` and filled by `pcbnew.ZONE_FILLER`:

* fill takes **0.42 s** and adds **444 polygon vertices**;
* `kicad-cli pcb drc --severity-all` then reports **0 violations and 0 unconnected
  items**;
* it runs with **no display and no X server** — `pcbnew` is headless-clean on
  9.0.8, which makes half of ADR 0001's stated reason for excluding it empirically
  stale. The other half — it forces a KiCad *runtime* into CI, where today only the
  `kicad-cli` binary is needed — still stands, and is the real cost of Option 1.

### 4. Per-pad-instance keying survives the shared-UUID defect

M10 requires per-instance keying twice (the `thermal`/`solid` override, and
stitching-via obstacle checks), and `docs/roadmap.md` records a defect that looks
like it would block both. Verified before relying on it, rather than assumed.

**The defect is real, and worse than the roadmap's wording suggests.**
`examples/usb-port` emits **31 pads carrying only 20 distinct UUIDs** — the Micro-B
receptacle's twelve pads numbered `6` all share one UUID, twelve times over.

**It blocks neither M10 requirement**, for two independent reasons:

* `route.obstacles` resolves a pad reference to a concrete instance via a
  `reference#index` key (`J1.6#7`, `J1.6#9`, …) built while walking the footprint's
  pad list — never via the UUID. Those keys are already distinct for the colliding
  pads and appear in the router's own output, so stitching-via obstacle checks
  inherit correct per-instance keying for free.
* KiCad's per-pad zone-connection override, `zone_connect`, is a token *inside* the
  pad's own s-expression — positional, not referential; stock footprints such as
  `L_Coilcraft_LPS4018.kicad_mod` carry it. A `thermal_pad` role can therefore be
  written onto one specific pad instance with no UUID lookup anywhere.

What stays broken is what was already recorded: a DRC violation landing on one of
twelve identically-identified pads cannot say which. Out of scope here, unchanged
by M10, still deserving its own milestone.

invoked directly on the same built board. The route and ERC+DRC columns are

What the table shows, which was not obvious beforehand:

* **`kicad-cli` is not where the time goes.** ERC, DRC and gerber export together
  cost about **1.05 s** on every board — remarkably flat, from the 4-connection
  `congestion` to the 41-connection `qfn-fanout`. On the two heaviest boards KiCad
  is 6% and 11% of `check`.
* **Routing dominates, and superlinearly.** `mcu-4layer` spends **9.78 s** routing
  against a 2.85 s mean build; `qfn-fanout` 5.75 s and `enclosure` 4.52 s. Nine of
  the twenty-six seconds of total routing time are one board.
* **A fixed ≈2.7 s floor sits under everything.** `aipcb validate` costs 1.73 s on
  the smallest example against 0.28 s of interpreter and CLI startup, so ~1.5 s is
  real work — resolving parts against KiCad's on-disk symbol and footprint
  libraries. Every command pays it. It is the largest single cost on six of the ten
  examples and nobody has looked at it.
* **The fill stage will be cheap.** 0.42 s measured on `usb-port`, against a
  ~1.05 s existing KiCad bill and a 7.2 s `check`. Fill is not the thing to worry
  about; a `pcbnew` interpreter start (0.076 s to `import pcbnew`) is noise.

## What the next session needs to know

**If the decision is to proceed** (ADR 0009, Option 1 or 2), the milestone is
otherwise unobstructed. The findings above remove the two unknowns M10 opened with,
and Finding 4 clears the per-pad-instance keying worry that would otherwise have
needed resolving mid-flight. Recommended order once unblocked: M10a → M10b →
M10d → M10c, i.e. get one pour filled and DRC-clean on one example before adding
either stitching or the integrity analysis, since both consume filled geometry.

**M11 depends on this.** M11 (high-speed: reference-plane checks) builds *directly*
on the filled zones from M10. It cannot start until this decision is made — a
reference-plane check over a board with no plane copper is checking nothing. This
is a chain stop, not a local one.

**Two things to watch when M10 does get built**, neither investigated here:

* The determinism measurement covers only zones with no thermal spokes and no
  island removal, because the probe boards had neither. `connect: thermal` and
  `remove_islands` are exactly where a fill tie-break would show up, so the
  measurement should be repeated on a board that exercises both before the
  stability policy is written into `docs/format.md` as claimed behaviour.
* `aipcb export` currently plots gerbers straight from the built board. If pours
  land without the fill stage landing at the same time, that path starts emitting
  boards with no plane copper and no warning — silently, as measured in Finding 1.
  The two must ship together.
