# M10 — copper pours, stitching vias & plane integrity: delivery report

**Delivered.** M10 stopped once, on 2026-08-21, because `kicad-cli` 9.0.8 cannot
fill a zone and the milestone's own guardrail said to stop rather than work around
it. The project owner accepted [ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-pours.md) Option 1 —
drive KiCad's own filler through a `pcbnew` subprocess, with a version lock — and
this report replaces the stop report with the milestone built on that decision.
**The stop is kept, in "The stop, and what it measured"**: the measurements that
caused it are the reason the implementation looks the way it does, and the
performance table it produced is the pre-pours baseline this one is compared
against.

## Deviations, stated loudly

Everything not delivered as the specification wrote it, in one place and at the top.

| Item | What happened |
|---|---|
| "All existing examples grow a `pours:` block" | **8 of 10.** `congestion` and `overconstrained` declare four signal nets and no ground at all — they are routing-topology fixtures, not boards, and pouring one of their signal nets would be a fiction. Recorded in [`docs/roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) and in ADR 0009 Finding 10. They are also the live witness for backward compatibility: `congestion` still builds with no zone in its output. |
| "pads with `role: thermal_pad` (or an explicit list)" | **The explicit list, not a role.** The source format has no per-pad object to hang a role on, and inventing one purely to name a pad is more schema than the problem needs. `pad_connect:` takes pad references instead — `U2.4` for every pad numbered 4, `U2.4#2` for the second one alone. The specification allows this ("or an explicit list"); it is flagged because a reader looking for `role: thermal_pad` will not find it. |
| A "deliberately fragmenting example" | **Two halves.** The exact island count and largest-island fraction are asserted on a synthetic board in `tests/test_pours.py` (one pour, one slice, 18×8 mm and 18×4 mm pieces, two thirds contiguous), because only a board built for it can have a *known* answer. The `min_contiguous` warning fires on a real example, `qfn-fanout`, where a 0.5 mm-pitch escape field genuinely cuts the back plane into five pieces. No example was fragmented on purpose by adding a track. |
| Keepout zones | **Built, but untested against a bundled example.** A pour now emits `layout.placement.keepouts` as KiCad keepout zones so the *filler* honours them, not only the router. No example exercises it, because the only two designs with keepouts are the two without a ground net. It has its own test with a purpose-built design. |
| Suite runtime | **Up from 10 m 58 s to 13 m 57 s** (968 tests, up from 886). Checking a board with pours now runs KiCad's filler twice — once as shipped and once with island removal disabled, so the report can say what removal deleted — and `tests/test_pours.py` fills eight examples of its own. Measured, not estimated; the breakdown is in the performance section. |
| A new prerequisite | **Filling needs KiCad's Python module**, not only the `kicad-cli` binary — the `kicad` package rather than `kicad-cli` alone. This is the cost ADR 0009 accepted, and it is confined: a design without `pours:` never invokes it, and the tests that need it skip cleanly when it is absent. Not a deviation from the specification, but a change in what CI has to install, so it belongs at the top rather than in a footnote. |
| `aipcb check --no-route` | **Checks a different board, and now says so louder.** A pour on a board with no copper on it fills differently, and on `enclosure` that produced a `starved_thermal` error the routed board does not have. Fixed in the source rather than in the test, by flooding the receptacle's shield tabs — which is what a shield tab wants anyway — but the general point stands: `--no-route` is not a cheaper version of `check`, it is a check of a different board. |

Nothing else was descoped. Every acceptance item in the specification is exercised
by a test or by a measurement in this report.

## What was verified

### The acceptance bar, per example

Eight examples gained a `pours:` block; four of them gained `stitching:` as well.
Every one builds byte-identically with its zones unfilled, fills through KiCad's own
engine, and passes `kicad-cli pcb drc --severity-all --schematic-parity` with **zero
violations, zero unconnected items and zero parity issues** on the *filled* board.
The numbers are in the acceptance table under "Performance", measured in the same
pass as the timings.

Three claims in that table are worth calling out because they are the ones the
milestone actually turns on:

* **`filled_polygon` in build output: 0, on every example.** That is the stability
  policy as a property of the file rather than as a promise.
* **F.Cu Gerber records, unfilled → filled.** On `usb-port`, 282 → 1758 with the
  board routed, and 31 → 1222 on the unrouted build output. An unfilled pour exports
  as no copper at all and looks exactly like a correct plot, which is why this is
  measured rather than asserted.
* **DRC violations after fill: 0.** The number only means anything after the fill:
  DRC over an unfilled pour has looked at no plane copper whatsoever, so a zero
  there would have been a zero about nothing. Four of these boards *did* report
  `starved_thermal` errors on the way here, and fixing them is what the thermal
  finding below is about.

### The split plane

`examples/mcu-4layer` is the four-layer case. `In1.Cu` carries the ground pour
declared with the outer layers; `In2.Cu` carries two: `VCC` over the whole layer at
priority 0, and a `GND` rectangle in the south-east corner at priority 1, which is
what "split plane" means — two nets, one layer, and an explicit rule about who wins
where they overlap. After the fill, measured off the board:

| Pour | Layer | Islands | Largest island |
|---|---|---|---|
| `VCC` | `In2.Cu` | 1 | 100% of the copper, 84.1% of the scope |
| `GND` region | `In2.Cu` | 1 | 100% of the copper, 99.5% of the scope |

Both planes contiguous, which is the acceptance wording, and asserted as a test
rather than only reported here.

The two inner pours are connected without a single track reaching them: the ATtiny85
is a DIP-8 and the ISP header a pin header, so their through-hole barrels pass
through both inner layers, and the `GND` grid stitching ties the region on `In2` to
everything else. Neither of those was designed for — it is what the example already
was — but it is the reason the board comes back with zero unconnected items.

### Thermal relief, read off the copper

The specification asks for this to be verified by reading the filled board, not by
trusting the parameter that asked for it. Around a thermally relieved pad the fill
leaves an annulus broken by its spokes, so the *gaps* in that annulus are countable:

| Pad | `zone_connect` | Gaps in the 0.25 mm annulus |
|---|---|---|
| `R1.2`, named in `pad_connect` as `solid` | 2 | **0** |
| `R2.2`, the zone's `thermal` default | *(none)* | **4** |

Two identical 0603 pads on one net, on one board, poured by one zone — so the only
thing that can explain the difference is the override. On the real example the same
comparison is made between two shield tabs of the same size: `usb-port` floods
`J1.6#7` and leaves `J1.6#6` thermal, and the flooded one has 85% of its own pad
area covered by pour copper against the thermal twin's 42%.

### Per-pad-instance keying against the shared-UUID defect

`docs/roadmap.md` records that pads sharing a number share a UUID. Re-verified
against the code this milestone actually wrote, rather than taken from the earlier
session's finding:

* `examples/usb-port` still emits **12 pads numbered 6 carrying one UUID between
  them**. The defect is unchanged and un-worked-around.
* The source names `J1.6#7`. In the built board **exactly one** of those twelve pads
  — the seventh — carries `(zone_connect 2)`. Asserted as a test that also asserts
  the twelve still share a UUID, so if the defect is ever fixed the test says so
  rather than passing quietly.
* Stitching's obstacle checks go through `route.obstacles`, whose keys are
  `reference#index` and never the UUID, exactly as Finding 4 predicted. Nothing in
  `route/stitch.py` reads a pad UUID.
* Going through the fill does **not** change this: `pcbnew` round-trips the board
  and the 31 pads still carry 20 distinct identifiers.

The defect blocks neither M10 requirement. It still blocks what it always blocked —
a DRC violation landing on one of twelve identically-identified pads cannot say
which — and that is out of scope here.

### Stitching

Four examples stitch. `usb-port` carries both of the common patterns — a 5 mm grid
over the plane and a 4 mm row round the rim at 1.5 mm inset. `enclosure` carries the
edge pattern alone, on a board with a radiused corner and a cutout, which is where
following an outline differs from following its bounding box. `mcu-4layer` and
`qfn-fanout` carry grids, for opposite reasons: one to make three ground pours into
one conductor, the other to rescue the back-plane islands a 0.5 mm-pitch escape
field leaves behind.

The `ring` pattern has no example. It shields a noise source, and none of the
bundled designs has one worth fencing; it is covered by a test that asserts every
via lands on the circle it named.

* Positions are **deterministic**: two independent builds produce the same UUIDs at
  the same coordinates, asserted as a test.
* A second `aipcb route all` into the same directory produces a **byte-identical**
  board — the generator strips its own previous vias by derived UUID before the
  router looks at the board at all, so this run's routing cannot depend on last
  run's stitching.
* Skipped positions are silent and counted: `usb-port` places 69 and skips 17,
  `qfn-fanout` places 63 and skips 27, `mcu-4layer` places 48 and skips 15,
  `enclosure` places 34 and skips 4. The counts are in the check report and under
  `summary.stitching` in `--json`; the skips themselves are not diagnostics, because
  a pattern generator dropping a blocked position is what it is for.
* Stitching vias are ordinary vias. After `aipcb route all` and then `aipcb build`,
  all 73 vias survive the rebuild — they are preserved like any other copper a
  rebuild did not generate.

### The fill subprocess

* **The version lock fires.** Asked to fill against a declared `kicad-cli` version of
  8.0.6 while `pcbnew` reports 9.0.8, the run stops with a message naming both and
  writes nothing. The subprocess enforces it independently of its caller: invoked
  directly with `--require-version 1.2.3` it exits 4 with "refusing to fill".
* **A Debian revision is not a mismatch.** `kicad-cli version` says `9.0.8` and
  `pcbnew.GetBuildVersion()` says `9.0.8+dfsg-1` on the same installation, so the
  comparison is on the numeric prefix. A lock that fires on a correct configuration
  is a lock people learn to disable.
* **`pcbnew` absent is loud.** Pointed at an interpreter that cannot import it, the
  fill raises with a message naming the package to install and the environment
  variable to override, and `aipcb check` reports `zone-fill-failed` **and does not
  run DRC**. That last part is the point: a check that fell back to the unfilled
  board would report a clean plane that is not there.
* **The interpreter is probed, not assumed.** Each candidate in a documented order
  is asked to import `pcbnew`; the first that can is used. On this machine that is
  `/usr/bin/python3`, and the project venv cannot (`include-system-site-packages =
  false`).

## The empirical findings

The two the specification asked for before writing code were measured by the session
that stopped, and are in ADR 0009 as Findings 1–3 with the stop report's summary
preserved below. Six more came out of building the thing; all are in
[ADR 0009](../decisions/0009-pours.md) under "Implementation findings", and these
are the ones that changed a decision:

**The fill geometry repeats; the filled file does not.** Finding 3 said the whole
file was byte-identical across runs. Re-measured on the shipping code — five fills
of `usb-port` in five separate processes — the fill polygons are identical every
time and the **whole file differs every time**: exactly 12 lines, all `uuid` tokens
of `Datasheet` and `Description` properties KiCad's writer *adds* to footprints that
lack them, each with a fresh random identifier. Strip every `uuid` token and the
five hash identically. That is why the stability policy in `docs/format.md` is
stated as "the guarantee covers unfilled build output" rather than the looser form
the stop report proposed. No UUID `aipcb` emits is lost: 279 go in and 279 come out,
so DRC on the filled board still maps every violation back to source.

**KiCad's own thermal-relief default produces boards KiCad rejects.** Emitting
KiCad's dialog defaults (0.5 mm gap, 0.5 mm bridge) produced `starved_thermal`
errors on four of the eight examples: on a 1.7 mm through-hole pad at 2.54 mm pitch
only one of four spokes can reach the plane, and KiCad 9 wants two. Measured on
`routing-demo`: 0.5/0.5 → 2 errors, 0.3/0.4 → 1, 0.25/0.5 → 0. `aipcb`'s default is
now 0.25/0.5, documented as a deliberate divergence, because a default that produces
an error is not a default.

**Same-net copper may touch; same-net holes may not.** The router's obstacle model
correctly lets a `GND` via overlap `GND` copper, so nothing stopped a stitching via
landing 0.2104 mm from a routed via on `mcu-4layer` against KiCad's 0.25 mm
hole-to-hole minimum. Stitching now checks every candidate against *every drilled
hole on the board*, whatever its net, on top of the copper-clearance check it
inherits.

**A stitching via has to land in copper.** A via on `GND` that touches no other
`GND` copper is an isolated island and KiCad reports it as unconnected — so a
candidate must sit inside the net's pours on **both** layers the barrel joins. That
is also what makes stitching useful: on `qfn-fanout` the escape field left the
receptacle's shield tab connected to an isolated island, which DRC reported as a
starved thermal; a 4 mm grid tied those islands to the front pour and the error went
away.

## Decisions, and why

**The zone is emitted; the fill is not.** `compile/zones.py` writes a boundary and a
set of rules and never a `filled_polygon`. Everything else follows from that: build
stays a pure function of the source, the fill is a derived artefact, and the one
thing that could make the two disagree — a stale fill in a file somebody rebuilt —
cannot exist.

**Fill happens in a staged copy, never in place.** `aipcb check` and `aipcb export`
copy the whole project — board, project file, schematic, library tables — into a
staging directory and fill the copy. The project file matters as much as the board:
a board checked without its `.kicad_pro` is checked against KiCad's default design
rules rather than the ones the source asked for, and it would pass.

**A fill that fails stops the check.** It is tempting to fall back to the unfilled
board and carry on. That is precisely the silent corruption this must not do: an
unfilled pour is indistinguishable from a filled one to every downstream tool, and
exports as no copper at all. `aipcb check` reports `zone-fill-failed` and reports no
DRC verdict.

**Stitching candidates are a function of the source, not of the copper.** The
lattice, the edge row and the ring are computed from the zones and the outline
alone; only which candidates *survive* depends on the tracks in the way. That is
what lets a second run recognise its own vias by derived UUID and replace them,
which is what keeps `aipcb route all` byte-stable when run twice into one directory.

**Island removal is measured, not inferred.** Saying "island removal deleted copper"
honestly means comparing the plane against itself with removal switched off. The
fill subprocess does exactly that, in the same process, when the check asks for it —
one extra `Fill()` call rather than a second interpreter start. Computing it
geometrically instead would have meant reimplementing part of the filler, which
ADR 0009 rejects.

**`min_contiguous` compares against the copper, not the scope.** A plane on a busy
board never fills its whole scope — pads, tracks and clearances take a third of it
on a dense two-layer board — so a scope fraction says more about component density
than about whether the plane is in one piece. Both numbers are reported; the
threshold uses the fragmentation one, and `docs/format.md` says so.

## Defects found

Four, all fixed, all recorded as ADR 0009 findings rather than left in the code.
Three surfaced because a new example was the first board to exercise the case; the
fourth surfaced because a test asserted a count:

* **A stitching via drilled 0.2104 mm from a routed via** on `mcu-4layer`. Copper
  clearance does not cover hole-to-hole, and same-net copper is allowed to touch.
* **An oval pad drill crashed the hole check.** `(drill oval 0.9 1.2)` is a slot, not
  a number; it is now measured across its long axis. Found by `enclosure`,
  `mcu-4layer` and `usb-port` simultaneously, which is what a shared helper does.
* **A generated zone would have been duplicated by a rebuild.** `zone` was in
  `PRESERVED_ITEMS` — everything in that set was, by definition, never generated
  before M10 — so an incremental build would have carried the old zone over *and*
  emitted the new one. Caught by `test_hand_drawn_zones_survive`, which counted
  zones and found two. The merge now skips an existing item whose UUID the fresh
  board already has, which is a no-op for every board built before this milestone.
* **Our thermal-relief default produced boards KiCad's own DRC rejects.** Emitting
  KiCad's dialog defaults was the obvious thing to do and was wrong on four of the
  eight examples; see the finding above. Worth counting as a defect rather than only
  a finding, because "match KiCad's default" is exactly the kind of decision that
  looks safe and is not.

## Deliberately not built

Recorded in [`docs/roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md):

* **Pour-aware routing** — return-path preservation and fragmentation-avoiding
  costs. Research: the router's cost model is a per-layer congestion field and a
  plane's connectivity is a global property of finished copper, so it is not a term
  you can add. The integrity report is M10's answer.
* **Current-capacity analysis of pours as conductors.** Needs current distribution
  over a shape rather than a width.
* **Reimplementing zone fill** — rejected, not deferred, in ADR 0009.
* **Making the *filled* board byte-stable.** Would mean emitting `Datasheet` and
  `Description` properties ourselves, changing the bytes of every board this tool
  has ever written, to fix something nothing depends on.
* **KiCad keepout zones as a source concept.** `layout.placement.keepouts` now
  reaches the filler, but a keepout is still a router idea in this format; a pour
  region is the positive form and covers what the examples needed.

## The stop, and what it measured

This milestone stopped on 2026-08-21 and delivered nothing but measurements. That is
part of its record, not a footnote: the guardrail said *"If KiCad's CLI cannot fill
zones headlessly in the installed version, stop and present options before working
around it"*, and it could not.

**`kicad-cli` 9.0.8 has no fill surface.** Six top-level commands, three under `pcb`
(`drc`, `export`, `render`), no fill subcommand, no fill flag, and no jobset job
that fills. Tested positively rather than inferred from `--help`, with a board
carrying two `GND` vias 24 mm apart and one unfilled `GND` zone spanning both — if a
stage fills, the unconnected count falls from 1 to 0:

| Stage | Unconnected | Filled? |
|---|---|---|
| `kicad-cli pcb drc --severity-all`, unfilled zone | **1** | no |
| the same, with the zone's `(fill yes …)` flag set | **1** | no |
| `kicad-cli pcb drc` after `pcbnew.ZONE_FILLER` | **0** | yes |

Gerbers answered identically: **596 bytes / 2 coordinate records** unfilled against
**1066 / 20** filled. An unfilled pour exports as nothing, without a warning. That
measurement is why `aipcb export` fills before it plots, and why a failed fill is an
error rather than a fallback.

**The determinism measurement** the specification asked for is Finding 3 in
ADR 0009, and it is the one this milestone had to correct: see Finding 5 above and
in the ADR.

**Nothing was implemented during the stop.** The decision that unblocked it, and the
four conditions attached to it — the recorded rationale, the version lock, the
framing as a bridge to be replaced when a future `kicad-cli` can fill, and the
subprocess hygiene — are in ADR 0009 under "The decision", and all four are
implemented and tested here.

## What M11 needs to know

M11 is high-speed: reference-plane checks, built directly on the filled zones from
this milestone. What it inherits:

* **`analyse_planes(board, netlist)` is a pure function of a parsed filled board.**
  It already gives per-layer islands with areas and bounding boxes. A reference-plane
  check wants the *shape* of the copper under a track rather than its island count,
  so the natural extension is to expose the unioned per-layer geometry rather than
  only its summary — the union is computed and then thrown away today.
* **`CheckResult.filled_board` is the path DRC actually ran against**, and the fill
  is staged rather than in place, so a second analysis pass costs a parse and not
  another fill.
* **Fill costs 0.70 s per board and the island-comparison pass adds another 0.5 s.**
  If M11 needs its own fill, reuse the check's rather than adding one; the staged
  filled board is right there.
* **Open question: nothing checks that a `stackup.planes` declaration and a `pours:`
  block agree.** A design could reserve `In1.Cu` for `GND` and pour `VCC` on it, and
  today only DRC would notice. That is a validation gap M11's checks make worth
  closing.
* **Open question: `min_contiguous` has no analogue for "is there plane under this
  track".** Fragmentation is measured over the whole pour; a reference-plane check
  cares about the copper under one net's run. The island geometry supports it; the
  reporting shape does not yet.
* **A plane is not necessarily one piece.** `qfn-fanout`'s back-side ground comes
  back in five islands on purpose. A reference-plane check that assumes "the plane"
  is a single polygon will be wrong on a real board; the island list is there
  precisely so it does not have to assume.
* **The router still knows nothing about pours.** Copper is poured after routing, so
  a track that crossed a plane and cut it in two did so without being told. That is
  the recorded out-of-scope item, and M11's checks are the first consumer that will
  notice.
* **`stackup.planes` and `pours:` are two different statements.** The first is
  routing law (nothing may route there); the second puts copper there. `mcu-4layer`
  declares both and they agree, but nothing enforces that they must, and a
  reference-plane check should read the pours rather than the plane declarations.

`docs/reports/` holds `m8.md` and `m9.md`, both written retroactively after the
report requirement arrived with M10, plus the orchestrator's own log and the
`chain-m10-m12.md` record of the halt. Nothing in this milestone needed
reconstructing from git archaeology: the stop report and ADR 0009 carried
everything.
