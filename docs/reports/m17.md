# M17 — router improvements, part 2: the measured candidates

M16 built the instrument and deliberately spent nothing on quality. This milestone
spends the evidence that instrument produced, and nothing else: three candidates the
baseline had already ranked, each with a runtime budget stated before it was built,
each landed against `bench --compare` on the whole corpus. No literature, no new
ideas — those are [M18](../roadmap.md#m18--the-literature-survey).

Everything below was measured on 2026-08-24 on the development machine (16 cores,
30 GB RAM, Linux 7.0.0-29-generic), CPython 3.14.4, Shapely 2.1.2 / GEOS 3.13.1,
KiCad 9.0.8 — the same machine and the same versions the M16 baseline was taken on,
which is the only reason the wall-clock comparisons below mean anything. Nothing
else was running during a timing run.

## 1. M17a — the vias the finished board did not need

### 1.1 What was built, and why it is one thing rather than two

`src/aipcb/route/tidy.py`, a post-pass over the routed board, running after
tightening and before the escape settling and the hole merging (both of those reason
about which vias are load bearing, so they should reason about the ones this leaves).

The unit of work is a **span**: legs *i* through *j* of one connection with the same
layer at both ends and vias in between. If that span tightens as a single leg on that
layer, the vias in it were bought and not used. Two shapes of candidate are offered:

* a span in the middle of a connection — the route left a layer and came back;
* the **whole connection**, once per copper layer both its pads can be reached on —
  because a connection whose two pads share a layer never *had* to change layer at
  all. (This is the complement of the baseline's `via_lower_bound`, which counts the
  connections where no such layer exists.)

A span is collapsed only when it is **strictly better on both axes**: at least one
via fewer, and not one micron more copper. There is no threshold to tune and no way
for the pass to make a board worse; the worst case is that nothing moves.

| ldo-supply | 0 | 0 | 0 | — | 0 → 0 | 0 → 0 |
| led-blinker | 1 | 0 | 0 | — | 1 → 1 | 1 → 1 |

The two collapses, both on GND:

```
GND U1.17>U1.49 gave back a retrace, 2 vias and 17.116 mm of copper on F.Cu
GND U1.49>U1.42 gave back 2 vias and 13.169 mm of copper on F.Cu
```

Corpus copper: **3 927.435 mm → 3 897.150 mm** (−0.77%). Every board except
`pcie-sata` is byte-identical.

### 1.3 The finding that matters more than the four vias

The baseline's headline was *37 of 90 layer changes were made where no corridor was
half full*, and the roadmap flagged in advance that this was a proxy: half capacity
is a stated convention, and "no pressure anywhere on the connection" is not "no
pressure at the via". **M17a is the sharpening the roadmap asked for, and the proxy
was overstating the opportunity by an order of magnitude.**

Of the 55 candidate spans, 53 are rejected and the reasons are geometry rather than
policy:

| Why a span was rejected | Count |
|---|---:|
| the single-layer route exists and is **longer** — the via bought something | 21 |
| the free space on the target layer is **split in two**: there is no on-layer route at any price | 32 |
| **capacity** — the collapsed leg would over-subscribe a cut | **0** |

So the honest statement about this router's vias is not "a third of them are waste".
It is: **four of ninety are, and the rest are load bearing**, tested against the
geometry rather than against a congestion proxy. That number is also the most useful
thing this milestone can hand [C3, the detour pass](../notes/toporouter-postmortem.md#c3-a-detour-pass),
whose expected yield was 7–16% less copper on historical boards.

### 1.4 The capacity arbiter, and the fact that it never fired

M16a implemented special cuts — the second diagonal of every convex adjacent triangle
pair — and ADR 0014 Decision 2 deliberately left them out of the router's cost model.
This pass runs downstream of every routing decision, so it can have the tighter cut
set for free, and it does: the occupancy it reasons about is built by charging every
finished leg to **both** cut families, and a collapse is refused when it would leave
a cut both fuller than it is now and past what its width carries.

The test is deliberately *relative* rather than absolute. A corridor already
over-subscribed on a built board is the shared approximate field disagreeing with the
exact per-net stretcher that laid the copper — `pcie-sata` reports 43 such cuts while
being DRC-clean and crossing-free — so demanding absolute headroom would refuse every
collapse on precisely the boards that have vias to give back.

**And on this corpus it refused nothing.** Length rejected 21 spans, geometry 32,
capacity 0. The arbiter is exercised by
`TestViaReclaim::test_a_swap_that_would_over_subscribe_a_cut_is_refused` and its
positive twin — a synthetic one-layer field whose cuts are given a third of a
millimetre of capacity — rather than by any real board. That is a gap in the
*evidence*, not in the code, and it is stated here rather than left to be inferred
from a green suite.

### 1.5 Controlled impedance: excluded, and the exclusion is tested

The milestone says *excluded unless their class's rules still hold after collapse
(verify, don't assume — a via removal changes the geometry M11e audited)*. The
fail-safe reading is taken: a connection is skipped when its net class declares a
differential impedance, or when any of its legs is a coupled pair run. Re-deriving
M11d's standoff rule and M11e's audit inside a post-pass is a milestone of its own,
and a pass that got it subtly wrong would move copper the SI report has already
characterised.

`test_a_controlled_impedance_net_is_left_alone` routes `pcie-sata` for real, collects
every net whose class returns `controlled_impedance`, asserts the set is non-empty —
so the test cannot pass vacuously — and asserts no collapse touched any of them.

**And that half of the test passes for the wrong reason, which is worth saying.** On
this corpus the class guard is never *reached*: every controlled-impedance connection
on `pcie-sata` carries either three coupled legs and no via of its own, or one via and
no legs at all — the holes belong to the declared pair transition (M11c), which is
pattern-generated copper this pass does not look at. `skipped_controlled` is therefore
**0** on every example. So the test also builds the guard a candidate it has to
refuse: a synthetic connection on a controlled-impedance net with a 0.02 mm excursion
to B.Cu and straight back on open board, which any ordinary net would give up
instantly. The guard refuses it, and its two vias are still there afterwards.

### 1.6 Determinism, order stability, idempotence

* **Deterministic.** Connections are visited in canonical order (net, then the two
  endpoint names), spans left to right, and every decision is arithmetic over
  geometry that is already fixed. `test_routing_is_byte_stable` and
  `test_stretching_is_deterministic` pass unchanged.
* **Idempotent.** `test_the_via_pass_is_idempotent` routes `pcie-sata`, rebuilds the
  field and the obstacle set, and runs the pass a second time over the connections it
  already collapsed: **zero collapses, zero vias**. Asserted rather than argued,
  because the pass mutates what it is given, so convergence is a property of the code
  and not of the shape of the problem.
* **The pads it re-tightens against are recorded, not guessed.** `RoutedConnection`
  gained an `open_pads` field. A route normally treats even its own net's other pads
  as things to go round; a connection the repair pass rescued was given every pad of
  its own net, because running through one beats not connecting at all. Guessing the
  strict set made eight spans on `pcie-sata` report "the free area is split in two"
  for a route the board is carrying straight through it. With the real set recorded,
  those eight resolve into six honest "longer" answers and two real splits.

## 2. M17b — the retrace, and the case that was its acceptance test

### 2.1 The defect, restated

M16b's E2 guard was expected to find nothing on the corpus — "which is the point",
said the note — and found this on `examples/pcie-sata`: GND's `U1.17>U1.49`, joining
two pads 3.519 mm apart, laid **20.635 mm** of copper in three legs.

```
leg 0  F.Cu   9.833 mm   U1.17            -> via#0
leg 1  B.Cu   0.555 mm   via#0            -> via#1
leg 2  F.Cu  10.247 mm   via#1            -> U1.49
```

Legs 0 and 2 run back through the same vertices in reverse. The B.Cu excursion is
half a millimetre. Roughly eight millimetres of copper and two vias buy nothing at
all.

### 2.2 The fix, and why it is not a separate mechanism

A retrace between two legs on one layer *is* a span returning to its own layer. There
is no other way for one connection's copper to meet itself: a single funnel output is
simple by construction, so the only way two pieces of one connection can coincide is
for the route to have left the layer and come back. Every `legs-meet` finding is
therefore a candidate for §1's collapse, and the collapse is what removes it.

So M17b is not a second pass. It is the same pass, plus the M16b detector used as an
*input*: `self_crossings` runs before anything moves, and a span whose layer it names
is reported as a retrace rather than merely as a via nobody needed. That is how the
diagnostic above knows to say "gave back a retrace, 2 vias and 17.116 mm".

**Result on the acceptance case:** `U1.17>U1.49` is now one F.Cu leg of **3.519 mm**
— which is the straight line between the two pads, so there is nothing left to take.
`done.routed.self_crossings` is empty, `route-doubles-back` does not fire on any
example, and the connection is 17.116 mm shorter with two fewer vias.

### 2.3 The guard stays, inverted rather than deleted

* `TestSelfCrossingInvariant::test_the_corpus_carries_exactly_one_known_doubling`
  became `test_the_corpus_carries_no_doubling`. It still routes `pcie-sata` for real,
  and it now asserts the count is **zero** and that no `route-doubles-back`
  diagnostic was emitted. A fix with no guard on it is a fix until somebody changes
  the cost model.
* `tests/test_check_loop.py`'s `KNOWN_ISSUES` **lost** `route-doubles-back` for
  `pcie-sata`. It is removed rather than silenced, and the comment in its place says
  what happened, so the entry cannot be re-added by habit.
* The seven unit tests that pin what the detector considers a finding are untouched.

### 2.4 Budget

The whole `reclaim` stage on `pcie-sata` is **0.450 s** against that board's M16
routing time of 35.87 s: **+1.3%**, against a budget of +5%. No other board's metrics
moved at all, so nothing regressed anywhere.

## 3. M17c — where 83–94% of the time lived, and where it actually was

### 3.1 The profile, and the surprise in it

The baseline said tightening dominates: 94% of `pcie-sata`'s routing time in the
`tighten` stage. `cProfile` over one un-instrumented run of that board said something
more specific, and it was not what the stage name implies.

| What | cumulative, of 87 s profiled |
|---|---:|
| `_retry` → `_repair` → `build_field` | **57.5 s** |
| of which `field.py::_via_sites` | **54.7 s** |
| `geometry_for` (free space + CDT, per leg) | 13.9 s |
| `constrained_delaunay_triangles` (247 calls) | 4.4 s |

**Two thirds of the "tightening" time was the field builder, rebuilt from scratch for
each of the sixteen connections the shared field could not place** — and inside it, a
scalar loop. Per candidate via site, per layer, `_via_sites` built a fresh
`shapely.Point`, called `free.covers`, called `triangulation.locate` (which built
another Point and made a Python-level R-tree query), and asked GEOS for
`free.boundary` **again** before measuring a distance to it. On `pcie-sata` that was
1.9 million Point constructions, 1.05 million `covers` calls and 854 000 boundary
derivations.

That is the answer to the milestone's question — *is the stretcher asymptotically
optimal or constant-slow?* — for the part M17c was allowed to touch. It was neither
mysterious nor algorithmic. It was trips across the Python/GEOS boundary.

### 3.2 The three changes

None of them changes an answer; all of them change how many times it is asked.

1. **`_via_sites` is vectorised.** One `shapely.covers` and one `shapely.distance`
   per *layer* over the whole candidate array, against a boundary derived once, plus
   a new `Triangulation.locate_many` that batches the R-tree query for every point at
   once. The tie-break is preserved exactly — candidates are still tried in ascending
   triangle index — so a point on a shared edge still lands where it did.
2. **`inflate` is memoised.** It is a pure function of a hashable polygon and a
   margin, and the router asks for the same answer 45 000 times on `pcie-sata` with
   fewer than a thousand distinct arguments. `lru_cache`, 65 536 entries.
3. **The STRtree over the triangles is built in one call** (`shapely.polygons` on an
   array, not a comprehension of `Polygon(t)`), and **`_realize` builds each layer's
   geometry once per connection instead of once per leg** — a route that goes F.Cu,
   B.Cu, F.Cu was unioning, differencing and triangulating F.Cu's free space twice
   for the same answer.

### 3.3 The measurement

`tighten` seconds, M16 baseline against M17c alone (i.e. before M17a/b landed, so
that the two effects are not mixed):

| Board | M16 | M17c | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **pcie-sata** | 33.883 | **15.083** | **−55.5%** |
| **mcu-4layer** | 8.553 | **4.080** | **−52.3%** |
| enclosure | 4.079 | 1.870 | −54.2% |
| qfn-fanout | 5.013 | 2.459 | −50.9% |
| usb-port | 2.341 | 1.175 | −49.8% |
| overconstrained | 0.141 | 0.067 | −52.5% |
| congestion | 0.099 | 0.066 | −33.5% |
| routing-demo | 0.152 | 0.129 | −15.3% |
| led-blinker | 0.246 | 0.210 | −14.8% |
| diff-pair | 0.217 | 0.185 | −14.7% |
| ldo-supply | 0.188 | 0.163 | −13.0% |
| **corpus `router_seconds`** | **61.863** | **28.290** | **−54.3%** |

The two slowest boards are at −55.5% and −52.3% against a target of −25%. The small
boards move least, which is the expected shape: they have no repairs, so they never
paid for the second, third and sixteenth field.

**And the quality side of the acceptance condition is the strong half:** at this
point **all eleven board hashes were byte-identical to the M16 baseline** — same
copper, same vias, same completion, same crossings, same over-subscription counts.
`bench --compare` reported improvements on runtime and nothing else. A performance
change that moves a single coordinate is a quality change wearing a disguise; this
one moved none.

### 3.4 What was *not* done, and what it hands M18

No algorithm was replaced, as the milestone required. The profile does not scream for
one — it screamed for a batch call, and got it — but it does leave a question the
constant factors have now uncovered:

> With the per-point Shapely traffic gone, the largest single remaining cost is
> `geometry_for`: union the obstacles, difference them from the board, triangulate
> the result — **once per connection per layer**, from scratch, on a free space that
> differs from the previous connection's by one route's worth of copper. Nothing in
> this project knows whether that is avoidable. An incremental CDT would answer it;
> so might a different decomposition.

That is written into [the M18 brief](../roadmap.md#m18--the-literature-survey) as the
question the tightening-theory reading has to answer.

## 4. M17d — the extrapolation, re-fitted

Method, stated so the next re-fit is comparable to this one and not to a remembered
number: **log-log least squares of `router_seconds` against connections attempted,
over all eleven bundled examples.** The same three models M16 reported:

| Model | M16 exponent | M16 R² | M17 exponent | M17 R² |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| routing seconds vs **connections** | 1.72 | 0.94 | **1.70** | **0.94** |
| routing seconds vs total cuts | 2.61 | 0.82 | 2.57 | 0.81 |
| routing seconds vs **connections × cuts** | **1.12** | **0.97** | **1.10** | **0.96** |

**The exponents did not move, and that is the finding.** M17c harvested constant
factors and left the shape of the curve exactly where it was — routing cost is still
close to linear in connections times triangulation size, which is still what "tighten
each connection against a triangulation of the whole board" predicts. Whether that
shape is the right one is M18's question, and this table is the evidence that M17
did not answer it.

**The new capability-ladder number.** Extrapolating the connection fit past the
measured range — and it is an extrapolation, from eleven boards spanning 4 to 90
connections —

> a 900-connection board would take roughly **11.3 minutes** to route
> (675 s), where the same method on the M16 baseline gives **23.7 minutes** (1 419 s).

Both figures are computed from committed results files by the script above. The
extrapolation's own precision is worth naming: three full corpus runs of this code on
this machine put the same number between 675 and 691 s, so it is a *scale*, not a
stopwatch reading — an eleven-to-twelve minute board, not an eleven-point-three
minute one.

One caveat, recorded because a number that quietly changes meaning is worse than a
wrong one: **the M17 milestone brief cites the old figure as "≈ 31 min" and the M16
report says "roughly half an hour"; neither is reproducible from the committed
baseline by the method above, which gives 23.7 min.** The honest comparison is
therefore 23.7 → 11.3 min, both computed here, both from committed results files.
The roadmap's graduation section cites no figure and needed no edit.

## 7. What this milestone did not do

**No literature.** The guardrail was explicit and it held: every technique here comes
from a number the M16 baseline already measured. The reading list is
[M18](../roadmap.md#m18--the-literature-survey), which is documentation only.

**No algorithm replacement in the stretcher.** M17c is engineering — batch calls, a
memo table, one duplicated computation removed. The question of whether the funnel
per connection against a whole-board triangulation is the right *shape* is untouched
and is now the first thing M18 has to answer, with §3.4's profile as its input.

**No regenerated README images.** `tools/make_images.py` renders the README's
pipeline pictures from `examples/pcie-sata`, and that board moved — so
`docs/images/04-routed.png` and `docs/images/_check.json` show 42 vias and
1 108.092 mm where the board now has 38 and 1 077.807. They are left alone on
purpose: regenerating means a 3D render and four PNG rebuilds, which is a large
binary diff for a four-via difference nobody can see at README scale, and the images
document the *pipeline* rather than a measurement. The next milestone that changes
what the board looks like visibly should run the tool.

**No detour pass.** C3 keeps its general case and has lost its named customer. The 53
rejected spans in §1.3 are the first real evidence about how much copper is left on
this corpus for a more ambitious post-pass to find, and the answer looks like "less
than the historical 7–16% suggests" — which is worth knowing before spending 30% of
the runtime budget to find out.
