## What M8 was asked for

Turn the single-layer topological router from M7 into a genuine multilayer one:
layer choice, via placement and congestion decided together rather than as three
afterthoughts. Plus two M7 leftovers folded in — meander length-matching and pair
crossovers.

## What was built

| Piece | Where it lives |
|---|---|
| M8a — one constrained Delaunay triangulation per copper layer, via columns, per-cut capacity | `route/field.py`, `route/stack.py` |
| M8b — A\* over one graph spanning every signal layer, PathFinder negotiated congestion, priority and rip-up | `route/graph.py`, `route/negotiate.py`, `route/costs.py` |
| M8c — differential pairs as one object in the graph, meander length-matching | `route/pairs.py`, `route/diffpair.py`, `route/meander.py` |
| M8d — the stretcher takes via hops; realization, repair and emission | `route/plan.py`, `route/geometry.py`, `route/emit.py` |

Schema additions, all optional and all defaulting to M7 behaviour:
`net_classes[].priority`, `net_classes[].rip_up`, `net_classes[].layer_forbid`,
`stackup.planes`, `stackup.via_types`, `stackup.preferred_direction`.

Two new example boards: `examples/mcu-4layer` (the 4-layer sig/gnd/pwr/sig board
the specification asked for) and `examples/congestion` (the stress case: two 4-pin
headers wired in reverse across a single channel, which is provably unroutable on
one layer and routable on two).

The two acceptance boards the specification called out are the first and the last
of these: `led-blinker` and `usb-port` could not be routed at all on one layer —
a Micro-B receptacle's 0.65 mm pad pitch has no room for a 0.25 mm track with
0.2 mm clearance between two pad edges — and both now route completely, using one
and four vias respectively to escape.

Other verified claims *(measured at reconstruction)*:

* **A plane layer carries no signal.** `mcu-4layer` declares `In1.Cu` a GND plane
  and `In2.Cu` a VCC plane; its 140 track segments are 123 on `F.Cu` and 17 on
  `B.Cu`, and none on either inner layer.
* **Coupled pairs.** `usb-port` couples `USB_DM+USB_DP` at a 0.2 mm gap with 21% of
  each half as fan-out; `mcu-4layer` couples both of its pairs (14% and 23%
  fan-out); `diff-pair` couples `DIFF_N+DIFF_P` (5% fan-out).
* **Meander length-matching.** `usb-port` adds 1.591 mm of meander to `USB_DM` to
  meet its 0.150 mm skew budget, and the resulting board is DRC-clean.
* **Byte-stability.** Every example produces identical bytes on a second
  `route all`, and a re-run over an already-routed board replaces its own copper
  rather than duplicating it.
* **Test coverage.** `tests/test_multilayer.py` holds 53 test functions (56 cases after parametrisation) covering the stackup
  as routing law, cut capacity and its reversibility, via columns, priority and
  rip-up ordering, negotiation reporting, determinism under shuffled input order,
  DRC-cleanliness of the two new examples, and the cost model's documented defaults.

Recorded during M8 (ADR 0007): letting each via settle toward the straight line
between its neighbouring corners cut the detour on `mcu-4layer` from 11.5 mm of
copper across five vias to 6.2 mm.

## Decisions, and why

The full record is [ADR 0007](../decisions/0007-multilayer.md). The load-bearing
ones:

**Congestion became a number instead of a shape.** In M7 a finished route narrowed
the next net's gates — elegant, and impossible to undo. M8 makes it an explicit
per-cut `used`/`capacity`/`history`, so ripping a route up subtracts its demand and
nothing else. ADR 0007 calls this "the single most important change in M8", and it
is what makes PathFinder affordable: the negotiation runs on symbols, and geometry
is produced once at the end.

**A via is a column, not a point on two layers.** It is an obstacle on every copper
layer its barrel passes, whether or not it carries signal there. Forgetting that is
how a router puts an inner-layer track through a drill.

**The cut criterion is the realizability test.** `capacity = length + reference
clearance`, `demand = width + clearance`. The `+ clearance` is exact arithmetic, not
a fudge: inflating both sides of a gap by `c` removes `2c` from it while *n* tracks
need `n·w + (n+1)·c`, so one clearance comes back.

**The plan is advice, not instruction.** The search runs on a shared field inflated
by the widest clearance on the board; the tightening runs on a per-net field that
knows where copper actually landed. Each leg is tightened both through the plan's
gates and directly, and the shorter is kept — worth about a fifth of the copper on
the bundled examples *(recorded during M8)*.

**A failed connection gets one private field.** The shared field's approximation
bites hardest on a connector's shield tabs, which are all one net and all in each
other's way. A connection it could not place is retried on a field built for that
net alone, immediately rather than at the end — deferring it means retrying against
the most crowded board there will ever be.

**A pair is one object and stays on one layer.** `max_vias = 0`, because a paired
via column at the pair's own pitch is one piece of copper (a 0.6 mm via at 0.54 mm
pitch), and splaying out to via pitch and back is a distinct pattern with its own
impedance discontinuity that was not built.

## Defects found

* **Two rotation bugs, both invisible until a footprint was rotated.** KiCad stores
  a pad's angle *absolutely* — it already includes the footprint's rotation — so a
  generated copy that leaves the library's angles alone describes a part whose pads
  did not turn with it. And KiCad's rotation is counter-clockwise as drawn in a
  Y-down coordinate system, so the obstacle extractor's textbook rotation was its
  mirror. Either alone puts pin 1 where KiCad draws pin 2, and the router then
  lands copper on the neighbouring pad. `mcu-4layer` was the first board to rotate
  one, which is how they surfaced.
* **Keepouts had never been read by any router.** `layout.placement.keepouts` was in
  the format from M1 and nothing honoured it. The layered field has to, because a
  keepout is exactly the kind of obstacle a via column must respect — so the per-net
  geometry and `route check` read it now too.
* **A net's own pads had to start blocking it.** Copper may legally overlap its own
  net, but a track that clips a pad it is only passing leaves a crescent a few
  microns wide that KiCad reports as a copper sliver. Only the two pads a connection
  is landing on are open to it; the repair pass lifts the restriction when the
  alternative is not connecting at all.
* **One copper sliver survives**, on `examples/diff-pair`, where two GND tracks of
  one net diverge at a shallow angle. It is a KiCad *warning*, not an error, and it
  is the only DRC finding on any example. It is recorded in
  [`docs/roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) and named as a known issue in
  `tests/test_check_loop.py` rather than silenced.

## Deliberately not built

* **Pair crossovers.** Two parallel wires on one layer form a two-strand braid, and
  exchanging them requires exactly one crossing. Moving both halves to another layer
  is a *translation*, not a transposition — whichever layer the pair ends up on, the
  halves still have to cross on it. This is planarity, not an implementation gap.
  The mechanism that would work (one half diving to the adjacent layer and back) is
  a real impedance discontinuity in one half only, and was not built. A pair that
  needs a crossover is reported, with the observation that swapping the two pads at
  one end removes it.
* **Pairs changing layer**, for the via-pitch reason above.
* **Copper pours.** A declared plane layer is a reservation: nothing signal-carrying
  goes on it, and the nets that would have lived there are routed as tracks. Pours
  are M10.
* **`side: back` placement**, left warned-and-ignored as the specification said.
* **ILP/SAT global optimisation, learned routers, continuous via-position
  optimisation in the search** — all excluded by the specification.

`usb-port`'s `DEV_DM`/`DEV_DP` pair does not couple: its end pads are a 2.54 mm
header, so the fan-out cannot reach them at the pair's own pitch and the halves are
routed separately with a stated reason (`diff-pair-not-coupled`). That is the
fail-safe behaviour the specification asked to keep, working as intended, and
`tests/test_multilayer.py::TestAcceptance::test_the_pairs_on_usb_port_couple`
asserts exactly that outcome rather than papering over it.

## Open questions carried forward

* **Copper pours** are the obvious next thing: a plane that holds no copper limits
  what any impedance claim can mean. (M10.)
* **The sliver-trimming heuristic** trims a route where it runs *along* its own
  net's copper but not where the two diverge. (Recorded in `docs/roadmap.md`.)
* **Length matching beyond a pair** — matching a whole bus to a target — is not
  built; skew is measured and meandered within a pair only.
* **Via-in-pad, thermal vias and stitching** all wait on pours to have anything to
  land in.
