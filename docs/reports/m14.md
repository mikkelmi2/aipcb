# M14 — readable schematics, optional routing, and the external-router bridge

Two themes, five pieces. The generated schematics were electrically correct and
unreadable; they are now drawn by convention, from information the source already
carried and never spent. And manual routing, which had been a thing that *happened*
to a board, became a thing the source can *declare* — with a headless bridge to an
external router built on top of it.

Everything below was measured on 2026-08-22 on the development machine (16 cores,
30 GB RAM, Linux 7.0.0-29-generic), against KiCad 9.0.8 and Freerouting 2.3.0, on the
tree as committed. Where a number is a projection rather than a measurement it says
so in the same sentence.

## 1. The schematics

### 1.1 What was wrong, precisely

M2's bar was "correctness over beauty", and the grid it licensed was the right trade
in M2. It stopped being the right trade when `examples/pcie-sata` needed a human
review against the controller's reference design before fab.

The specific failure was not ugliness. It was that **every fact the source knew about
why a part exists died at the sheet boundary.** `role: decoupling`, `for: U1`, the
module hierarchy, the netlist graph — all of it was available and none of it reached
the drawing. A capacitor whose entire purpose is to sit at one pin of one IC was
placed by its position in a sorted list. On `pcie-sata` the mean distance from a
decoupling capacitor to the nearest pin of the IC it decouples was **157.5 mm**, on a
sheet 594 mm wide.

### 1.2 What decides placement now

`aipcb/compile/sheet.py`, in five steps:

1. **Satellites.** A component whose `role` is in the satellite set attaches to the
   component its `for:` names. A pull-up or pull-down with no `for:` attaches to the
   component sharing the non-rail net it biases — resolved deterministically,
   preferring a controller, then by reference designator.
2. **Blocks.** A module instance becomes one cluster, drawn with a dashed frame and
   its instance name. Everything else is its own block, carrying its satellites.
3. **Rank.** Blocks are layered left to right by breadth-first distance over
   **signal nets only**. Power and ground are excluded deliberately: they touch
   everything, and a graph in which everything is adjacent has no layers.
4. **Order within a rank.** The barycentre heuristic, swept forward and back twice,
   ties broken on block key.
5. **Geometry.** Extents measured from the symbols actually in use, including the
   room a power symbol or a net label needs, then columns packed and the drawing
   centred on the smallest paper that holds it.

**The step that was not obvious was (3)'s seed.** The first implementation seeded the
walk from every connector, which is what "connectors on the left" reads like. On
`pcie-sata` that put all four SATA ports in the same column as the PCIe edge
connector they are downstream of — the sheet said the opposite of what the board
does. The seed is now the connector that looks like an *input*: an `edge_connector`
if the source names one, otherwise whichever connector carries the most rail pins.
`tests/test_sheet.py::test_the_input_connector_is_left_of_the_controller` is that
case, written so it fails on the version that got it wrong.

### 1.3 Three things found by measuring rather than by looking

Each of these was found because the readability metric moved the wrong way, and each
was a real defect rather than a matter of taste.

**Stacked pins were drawn once per pin.** KiCad's own libraries stack a part's
repeated power pins on a single coordinate — `Connector:Bus_PCI_Express_x1` draws its
nine grounds as one pin, its five 12 V pins as another. Emitting a stub and a label
for each produced **nine identical `GND` labels in one place** — which accounted for
**all 21** of the overlaps still left on the `pcie-sata` sheet at that point. One point
carrying one net is one connection, and is now drawn once. Connectivity is
unaffected — every pin at that coordinate is joined by the one wire, which is exactly
why the library draws them that way.

**Reference and value text was placed from the pins rather than from the drawing.**
The text offset was computed from the pin field, so on a connector with nine ground
labels hanging below it the value landed across all nine. Text is now placed from the
*measured extent* of everything else the component draws, and the extent is then
widened to include the text — so the next component is placed clear of it too.

**Asymmetric blocks overflowed their column.** A block was centred in its column *by
its anchor's origin*. An IC with a row of coupling capacitors east of it has an
extent that is nothing like symmetric about its anchor — `U1` in `qfn-fanout` reaches
25.4 mm left and 46.2 mm right — so centring the anchor pushed 10.4 mm of it into the
next column, where it landed on that block's labels. Blocks are now placed by their
extent. This was the last two overlaps on the board.

Two more decisions came out of the same measurements:

* **Power symbols, but not on a crowded side.** A rail or ground pin gets a KiCad
  power symbol instead of a text label. But a ground symbol with its name beside it
  is wider than a 2.54 mm pin pitch, and standing one on every pin of a six-way
  header puts three net names on top of three others — worse than the labels it
  replaced. A side whose pins are closer together than a power symbol is wide keeps
  its labels.
* **Stub staggering was measured and then mostly turned off.** Staggering alternate
  stubs to make a staircase is the classic fix for a dense pin row, and it worked —
  but it cost `pcie-sata` 470 mm of extra wire (396 → 866 mm) to remove overlaps that
  the two fixes above removed for free. It now triggers only when pins are closer
  together than a label is tall, which on real parts is rare. `pcie-sata`'s wire
  length ended up **below** where it started.

### 1.3a Junctions, and why there are none

M14b asked for correct junction dots and for four-way junctions to be avoided. The
sheets emit **zero** `junction` nodes, and that is the correct answer rather than an
omission: in the netlist-first idiom no two wires ever meet. Every wire on the sheet
is a stub from one pin to one terminal, connectivity is carried by the name on the
end of it, and a junction dot exists to disambiguate a place where wires cross
without connecting — a place this sheet does not have. Four-way junctions are avoided
for the same reason, structurally rather than heuristically. KiCad's ERC agrees: 0
violations on all eleven, and it checks connectivity, not drawing style.

### 1.4 The guardrail: nothing electrical moved

The milestone's hard rule was zero electrical change. Three independent checks, all
run on all eleven examples:

**KiCad's own netlister, name for name.** `kicad-cli sch export netlist --format
kicadxml` was run against the pre-M14 golden sheets and the post-M14 sheets, and the
extracted netlists compared as `{net name → set of component pins}`, with `#`-prefixed
scaffolding filtered exactly as the M2 test has always filtered it.

```
congestion       netlist SAME     enclosure     netlist SAME     pcie-sata     netlist SAME
diff-pair        netlist SAME     ldo-supply    netlist SAME     qfn-fanout    netlist SAME
led-blinker      netlist SAME     mcu-4layer    netlist SAME     routing-demo  netlist SAME
overconstrained  netlist SAME     usb-port      netlist SAME
```

Comparing *names* and not just membership matters here, because power symbols now
name their own nets from their `Value` field. If that mechanism were ever untrue —
if a `power:VCC` graphic with `Value` `P3V3` produced a net called anything other
than `P3V3` — this is where it would show. It does not.

**ERC.** `kicad-cli sch erc --severity-all`: **0 violations on all eleven**, before
and after.

**The files themselves.** Regenerating the golden tree changed **eleven files, all of
them `.kicad_sch`**. No `.kicad_pcb` moved. No `.kicad_pro` moved. That is the
file-level statement of "presentation change only".

Two builds of an unchanged design remain byte-identical on all eleven.

**And every new element is accounted for.** M4's rule is that every UUID in a
generated file maps back to a line of source, and `tests/test_check_loop.py::
TestUuidIndex` enforces it. The new sheet elements broke it on nine of the eleven
examples the first time the suite ran — power symbols, their pins, and the module
cluster frames were all UUIDs nothing could explain. They are now registered: a power
symbol and its pin map to the same pin the label they replaced mapped to, the flag's
rail symbol maps to its net, and a cluster's frame and name map to the module
instance they draw. A generated element nothing can account for is exactly what that
index exists to make impossible, and it caught this within minutes.

Reading it honestly:

* **Overlaps: 71 → 0.** This is the metric that carries the argument. Five of the
  eleven had overlapping drawn items and now none does.
* **Wire crossings: 0 → 0, everywhere.** The milestone named this as "where most
  visual noise comes from", and on a wire-routed schematic it is. On a netlist-first
  one it is structurally zero: connectivity is names on short stubs, so there is
  almost nothing long enough to cross anything. Reporting it as evidence of
  improvement would be dishonest; it is reported as a metric that was already at its
  floor. The same applies to wires through symbol bodies.
* **Wire length: down 45 mm in total**, and down on the two largest sheets. Three
  examples went *up* by 2.5 mm each — one grid step, from a passive standing where a
  power symbol needs room — which is the visible cost of the convention and is not
  worth hiding.
* **Decoupling distance: down on every example that has a local capacitor**, by 63 %
  on average and by 82 % on `pcie-sata`. This is the number that says the source's
  intent reached the drawing.

  `routing-demo` shrank; both are small designs where a flow layout is inherently
  wider than a square grid. `pcie-sata` went from **A1 to A3** — a quarter of the
  paper, four times the density of meaning.

### 1.6 The review artefacts

`aipcb build --render` writes, beside the KiCad files:

```
review/pcie-sata.pdf        what a reviewer reads, plotted by kicad-cli
review/pcie-sata.svg        the same, for a browser or a diff
review/readability.json     the numbers above, for this sheet
```

It is a flag rather than the default because plotting costs about ten times what
generating the sheet does (§6), and most builds are not reviews. It writes to
`review/` and not `out/`: `out/` is `aipcb export`'s fabrication package, and a fab
package with review PDFs in it is a fab package somebody has to sort.

A missing `kicad-cli` is reported and skipped rather than raised — the renders are
review material, and a build that produced correct KiCad files should not fail
because the machine cannot plot them. The readability measurement still runs.

### 1.7 What a reviewer sees on the pcie-sata sheet

Committed as [`m14/pcie-sata-schematic.pdf`](m14/pcie-sata-schematic.pdf), A3, one
sheet. Read left to right:

* **The card edge, left.** `J1`, the `PCIE_X1_EDGE`. Its two rails leave the top of
  the symbol as `P12V` and `P3V3` arrows; its nine stacked grounds leave the bottom
  as one `GND` — one wire, one symbol, as KiCad's own library intends. `REFCLKP` and
  `REFCLKN` are labelled at the `REFCLK+/−` pins on its west face. `PCIE_TXP_C` and
  `PCIE_TXN_C` leave the `PETp0/PETn0` pins on its east face, and `PCIE_RXP/RXN`
  arrive at `PERp0/PERn0` just below them. The `PRSNT1/PRSNT2` and `JTAG1-5` pins
  carry no-connect crosses, which is the sheet saying *deliberately* rather than
  *forgotten*. `C1`, the 12 V bulk capacitor, stands directly beneath it with `P12V`
  above and `GND` below.
* **The controller, centre.** `U1`, `SATA_CTRL_QFN48`. Every one of its 48 pins is
  labelled down its west face in pin order, so the four SATA ports read as four
  contiguous groups — `SATA2` at pins 2–6, `SATA3` at 8–12, `SATA0` at 25–29,
  `SATA1` at 31–35 — with `REFCLKP/N` at 15/16 and the PCIe lane at 18/19 and 23/24
  between them. Its `P3V3` pins at 38 and 45 and its grounds are labelled on the same
  face.
* **The decoupling row, under the controller.** `C4` (10 µF), `C5` and `C6` (100 nF)
  in a row, every one of them standing the same way up, every one with a `P3V3` arrow
  above and a `GND` symbol below. Three capacitors, one glance.
* **The AC-coupling pair, between controller and edge.** `C2` and `C3`, in a column
  on the controller's east face, `PCIE_TXN`→`PCIE_TXN_C` and `PCIE_TXP`→`PCIE_TXP_C`
  in line — the discontinuity in the middle of the transmit pair, drawn as a
  discontinuity in the middle of the pair.
* **The four SATA ports, right.** `J2` through `J5`, stacked, identical in shape, each
  with its four signal labels and its mounting-hole ground. Reading down them, the
  port order is `SATA0, SATA1, SATA2, SATA3`.
* **The power band, top left.** Three `PWR_FLAG`s on `GND`, `P12V` and `P3V3`, each
  paired with its own rail symbol.

**Following REFCLK by eye**: `REFCLKP` at `J1`'s `REFCLK+` pin, `REFCLKP` at `U1` pin
15. The two labels are the only two instances of that name on the sheet, on the two
symbols the reviewer cares about, with nothing between them. That is what
label-based connectivity gives you and it is deliberate (ADR 0003) — a reviewer
follows the *name*, and the name is the one written in the YAML. What a reviewer
cannot do on this sheet is trace a drawn conductor from one to the other, because
there is not one. If that is wanted, it is a different ADR and a different milestone.

**What is spot-checkable against a datasheet**: the pin grouping on `U1` (contiguous
per port, in port order), the presence and count of decoupling per rail, the
AC-coupling capacitors on the transmit pair only, and the rail/ground assignment on
every one of `J1`'s 36 fingers. What is *not* checkable from this sheet is anything
about geometry — that is `aipcb check` and the M11e report's job, and the sheet does
not pretend otherwise.

`mcu-4layer`'s render is committed alongside it as a second, denser example.

### 1.8 What was not built, and why

**Multi-sheet output.** The largest bundled example has 12 components; the median has
7. Any component threshold worth configuring is far above that, so a hierarchical
splitter would have shipped with no design in the repository exercising it — which is
precisely the failure mode `CLAUDE.md` exists to prevent, one level up. The
groundwork is real: because ADR 0003 chose *global* labels, whose names have no sheet
scope, splitting symbols across sheets would not rename a single net. What is missing
is the child-sheet files, the `sheet` nodes, the per-sheet instance paths, and a
design big enough to prove them. It is on the roadmap with that sentence attached.

**Buses.** No bundled design declares a grouped construct that reads as a bus. The
two that exist — differential pairs and `max_skew_mm` classes — are two-net groups,
and drawing a two-net bus makes a sheet less readable, not more. Deferred until a
design has an actual byte-wide group.

## 2. M14c — the preserve finding, and the policy

### The session-context question, answered

**Does M6's preserve mechanism cover `.kicad_sch` at all, and what happens to manual
edits on rebuild today?**

**It does not cover it, and never did.** `merge_board` — the whole of ADR 0005's
UUID-and-fingerprint machinery — is called on the board and only the board.
`build_design` wrote the schematic with an unconditional `_write_if_changed`: no read
of the existing file, no merge, no diagnostic. Manual edits to a generated schematic
were silently overwritten on every rebuild, and nothing anywhere said so. That is the
"silent third thing" M14c named and forbade.

### The policy: (b), explicitly

**Manual schematic layout is not supported, and a rebuild that is about to discard
one says so.**

Option (a) — extending the fingerprint machinery to the sheet — was considered and
rejected on two grounds.

The weaker ground is that everything on a sheet is generated. Unlike a board, which
carries tracks, zones and dimensions that aipcb never emits, there is no class of
schematic element that is not ours, so "preserve the human's version" has nothing to
attach to except position.

The decisive ground is that after M14a, **the sheet's layout is a computed global
property**. Ranks come from the flow graph, order within a rank comes from a
barycentre sweep over the whole column, and every position depends on every extent
around it. Pinning three symbols where a human left them while the other forty re-flow
around them produces a drawing that is neither the human's nor the generator's — and
worse, one whose remaining automatic parts have been optimised against constraints
that are no longer true. On a board, position is engineering: impedance, mechanics,
thermals, and preserving it is obviously right. On a sheet, position is presentation.

So: **the YAML is the reviewable source; the sheet is a view of it.** Said in
`README.md`, in `docs/workflows.md`, and in the ADR 0003 amendment.

### The mechanism

Every generated sheet carries a hash of its own contents in title-block comment 9:

```
(comment 9 "aipcb-sheet:7b3fe1b66e4801fb")
```

Comment 9 because it survives KiCad's own round-trip, is not drawn on the default
worksheet, and needs no extension to the file format. The hash is taken over the
whole sheet with the stamp itself removed, on a copy, so measuring a tree never
changes it.

On rebuild, a sheet whose stamp no longer matches its contents has been edited:

```
warning[schematic-edits-discarded]: led-blinker.kicad_sch has been edited since aipcb
generated it, and those edits are about to be regenerated away
  hint: the schematic is a view of the design, not a second copy of it: move the
  change into the YAML, where it is reviewable and survives. Copy the sheet aside
  first if you want to keep it
```

A sheet with **no** stamp answers "unknown" rather than "edited" — it was written by
something else, or by a pre-M14 aipcb, and *"somebody edited this"* and *"I do not
know who wrote this"* are different sentences that deserve different words.

`tests/test_manual_routing.py::TestSchematicEditPolicy` is the policy test the
milestone required: it builds a sheet, moves a capacitor in the file, rebuilds, and
asserts both that the warning fired and that the edit is gone. It demonstrates the
policy doing exactly what it says.

## 3. M14d — `routing: manual`

### The field

On a net class it covers every net in the class; on a net it overrides the class in
**either** direction:

```yaml
net_classes:
  quiet:
    routing: manual
nets:
  SIG_A: { class: quiet }                    # manual, from its class
  SIG_B: { class: quiet, routing: auto }     # ...and back out again
```

The override going both ways is not decoration. The case it exists for is a mostly
hand-drawn group whose ground return you are happy to let the router lay.

### What the router does with it

Declared-manual nets are excluded **once**, in `route_board`, before anything is
paired, negotiated or sketched. A net skipped in one place and routed in another
would be worse than not having the field at all, so the exclusion is applied to the
pair finder, the sketch realizer and the connection list from one set.

### One thing it deliberately does not turn off

A *pattern generator* is not the router. Measured on `examples/qfn-fanout` with
`IO_PD0` declared manual: the negotiated router leaves the net alone, and the
**fan-out escape is still laid on it**.

That is a judgement call and it is recorded rather than made quietly. `fanout:` is an
explicit block asking for a specific escape shape on a specific package, exactly as
`pours:` and `stitching:` are; suppressing it because a net is declared manual would
silently disable the declaration the user wrote a whole block for. The other
direction — a user who declares a pair manual and finds stubs already on it — is a
real surprise too, so the overlap is reported:

```
info[manual-net-has-generated-pattern]: IO_PD0 is declared `routing: manual` and also
carries copper from a declared pattern (a fanout escape or a pair via transition)
  hint: a pattern generator is not the router: the source asked for that shape by
  name. The rest of the net is still yours
```

Documented in `docs/workflows.md` and the ADR 0006 amendment. If it turns out to be
the wrong call, it is one condition in one place.

### The four states

`aipcb check --json` and `aipcb route all --json` both carry `summary.nets`:

```json
{"counts": {"manual-pending": 1, "auto-routed": 4},
 "manual_pending": ["CROSS"],
 "nets": [{"net": "CROSS", "class": "signal", "state": "manual-pending",
           "declared": "manual",
           "reason": "declared `routing: manual` and has no copper yet"}, ...]}
```

`manual-pending` is the state the whole feature is for. To anything that counts
unrouted connections, a board whose critical pairs are declared-manual-and-undrawn
looks finished. It is now a named state, listed by net, and warned about by
`aipcb check`.

The state is read off the **board**, not tracked through the pipeline, because the
whole point of a manual net is that its copper may have arrived by a route this
program knows nothing about — a hand route in KiCad, or a session file from a router
aipcb never ran.

### The round trip the milestone asked for

Declared manual → routed → untouched and listed pending → routed externally → reported
manual-routed → rebuild preserves it. Measured end to end on `examples/mcu-4layer`
with its `isp` class declared manual:

```
aipcb route all      31 connections routed, PB0/PB1/PB2/RESET untouched
aipcb export --dsn   85 pieces of existing copper fixed; to route: PB0, PB1, PB2, RESET
freerouting          -de mcu-4layer.dsn -do mcu-4layer.ses      (exit 0, no display)
aipcb import --ses   25 pieces of copper spliced in, 174 kept, 0 drift
                     manual-routed: 4, auto-routed: 7
                     drc: 0 errors, 0 warnings, 0 unconnected items
aipcb build          kept manual work: 10 hand-placed footprints; 145 segment, 54 via
                     PB0, PB1, PB2, RESET still have copper
```

## 4. M14e Phase 0 — what was measured before anything was built

The hard gate, run before a line of bridge code was written, per the standing rule
that an ADR's premises about external tools have expiry dates.

| Question | Finding | How |
|---|---|---|
| Does `kicad-cli` 9.0.8 export DSN or import SES? | **No.** `kicad-cli pcb` offers `drc`, `export`, `render`; `export` has no Specctra format. | `kicad-cli pcb --help`, `kicad-cli pcb export --help` |
| Does `pcbnew` export DSN headlessly? | **Yes.** `ExportSpecctraDSN` returned true and wrote **47 508 bytes** for `examples/pcie-sata` with `DISPLAY` and `WAYLAND_DISPLAY` unset. | `env -u DISPLAY python3` probe script |
| Does `pcbnew` import SES headlessly? | **Yes.** `ImportSpecctraSES` took a hand-written session into an unrouted `examples/led-blinker`: tracks 0 → 1, board saved. | same |
| Does Freerouting have a working headless CLI? | **Yes.** `freerouting -de in.dsn -do out.ses` completed on `led-blinker.dsn`: fanout, auto-route and optimise stages all ran, exit 0, SES written. It warns `Couldn't get screen resolution. If you are running in a headless environment, disable the GUI...` and proceeds. | Freerouting 2.3.0 linux-x64 bundle, `env -u DISPLAY` |

So the bridge runs through the `pcbnew` subprocess boundary ADR 0009 already
sanctioned for zone filling: same rule (no `pcbnew` in aipcb's own process), same
version lock against `kicad-cli`, same standalone script importing nothing from
aipcb.

Two things Phase 0 also settled that were not on the list. Freerouting is **not
packaged for this machine and no JRE is installed**; the 2.3.0 `linux-x64` bundle
carries its own runtime, which is what made the verification possible without a
system change. And KiCad's DSN export writes existing copper as `(type route)`, which
is the first of the two ways to lose a board — §5.

## 5. M14e — the bridge, and the two ways to lose copper

Both were found by measurement. The second was found the hard way, by losing a board.

### On the way out: `(type route)` invites destruction

KiCad exports every existing track and via into the DSN's `wiring` section as
`(type route)` — Specctra for "the router may move this". On a hybrid board that is
exactly backwards: the point of sending a DSN is to have the *remaining* nets routed,
not to have M11's coupled pairs re-drawn by something that has never heard of
coupling.

Every wire and via is rewritten to `(type fix)` on the way out. In the `wiring`
section only: the same token appears in `structure` rules meaning something else, and
a global replace would corrupt the file. Measured on the routed `pcie-sata`: 189
pieces of copper, all of them fixed, no `(type route)` left in the file.

### On the way back: SES import *replaces*, it does not add

`pcbnew.ImportSpecctraSES` does not merge a session's routing into a board. It
**replaces** the board's routing with it.

```
imported -97 tracks and -52 vias into mcu-4layer.kicad_pcb
  manual-routed: 4
  unrouted: 7
```

That is the first working version of `aipcb import --ses`. Four nets got the copper
they were sent for and **seven nets lost the copper they already had**, including all
52 stitching vias. Nothing said so: the file parsed, the import returned success, and
`kicad-cli pcb drc` reported **0 errors** — because copper that is gone violates no
rule. A board would have gone to fab with seven nets missing and a clean DRC report
attached.

The fix is to stop trusting the import. The session goes into a scratch copy, and
only the copper for nets that were **actually pending** is lifted out of that copy and
spliced into the real board. Net codes are matched by *name* through each board's own
table, because the importer renumbers. Copper the session carried for a net the DSN
fixed is counted, ignored, and reported as `session-touched-fixed-nets`.

After the fix, the same round trip:

```
splice: {'taken': {'PB0': 4, 'PB1': 4, 'PB2': 2, 'RESET': 15},
         'ignored': {}, 'protected_kept': 174}
drift: []
still_pending: []
counts: {'manual-routed': 4, 'auto-routed': 7}
drc: {'ran': True}          # zero errors, zero warnings, zero unconnected items
```

25 pieces of copper in, 174 kept, nothing lost.

### And a third way, which was this milestone's own bug

The first `aipcb import --ses` ended by calling `check_design(design, route=False)` to
verify what had arrived — which reads as obviously right and was obviously wrong.
`check_design` builds **fresh** on purpose since M13.5, so that a check is a function
of the source alone. A fresh build of a design has *no copper on it at all*. The
import was therefore reporting DRC on an empty board:

```
drc: {'ran': True, 'warning': 1, 'info': 16}     # 16 "missing connection" items
warning[routing-manual-pending]: 4 nets declared `routing: manual` still have no
copper: PB0, PB1, PB2, RESET                    # ...on a board that had it
```

Zero errors, and meaningless — a verification step that could not fail. It is exactly
the shape of the two defects above, one level up: the process reported success while
measuring the wrong artefact, and nothing in the output said which board it had
looked at.

`check_design` now takes a `board_file`, used by `aipcb import` and by nothing else:
the fresh build still supplies the project file, the schematic and the library tables
DRC needs beside a board, and the copper comes from the board on disk. M13.5's
invariant is untouched — the check is still a function of the source plus the board
it was pointed at, and re-running it changes nothing. After the fix the same command
reports `drc: {'ran': True}` with no unconnected items, because the board it is now
looking at is actually routed.

`tests/test_manual_routing.py::test_the_check_after_import_sees_the_imported_copper`
is the test that would have caught it, and asserts the distinguishing symptom: the
net states the check reports must match the board, not an empty build of the source.

### Verified, not trusted

SES import reconstructs net classes from names, and reconstruction can be wrong. Every
track width and via size that comes back is compared against the class the *source*
declares, and any difference is reported as `session-geometry-drift` with both
numbers. On the Freerouting round trip above, **zero drift** — the widths and via
sizes it returned match the `isp` class exactly.

It is reported and never corrected. Silently widening somebody else's track would
make this bridge a router, which is the thing ADR 0006 declined to be.

### The rule with teeth

`aipcb export --dsn` warns when a declared-manual pending net is on a
controlled-impedance class:

```
warning[controlled-impedance-to-external-router]: PCIE_TXP, PCIE_TXN are on a
controlled-impedance net class and about to be handed to an external router
  hint: an external router knows nothing about coupling, skew, the reference plane
  or the derived pair geometry, and aipcb cannot check what it did not decide.
```

A warning and not a refusal: somebody may have a reason, and this program does not
know every board. But never quietly. Tested in
`test_a_controlled_impedance_pending_net_is_warned_about`, which declares `pcie_tx`
manual on a copy of `examples/pcie-sata` and asserts the code fires.

### What the external router actually managed

Reported because it is the honest picture of what this bridge buys, and it is not
uniformly good.

| board | fixed copper | asked to route | Freerouting's own verdict | after import |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `mcu-4layer` | 85 pieces | PB0, PB1, PB2, RESET | 4 unrouted, 19 violations (its model) | all four routed, **DRC completely clean**, 0 drift |
| `routing-demo` | 9 pieces | CROSS | 2 unrouted, 4 violations | not imported |

Two things worth saying about that first row. Freerouting's counts are against **its**
rule model on a board where 85 pieces of copper are immovable, and they disagree with
what KiCad's DRC made of the result — which is why `aipcb import` runs `check` and
reports *that* rather than relaying somebody else's score. And `routing-demo` is a
board that exists to be congested; aipcb's own router hands its nets over too. An
external router is not a way around a board that has run out of room.

### Explicitly not built

Stated in `docs/external-routers.md`, and refined in place in both
[ADR 0006](../decisions/0006-routing-approach.md) (the routing approach) and
[ADR 0008](../decisions/0008-mech-placement.md#rejected) (where the rejection was
actually recorded), rather than left to be inferred from an absent flag: no
`--engine`, no invocation of any router from aipcb, no log parsing, and no
promise about the output beyond `aipcb check`'s verdict. External copper is manual
copper — preserved per element, no source mapping, and **exempt from the determinism
bar**, because two Freerouting runs on one DSN differ and that is expected. The SES
file, not the run that made it, is the reproducible artefact.

## 8. What M14 leaves open

* **Multi-sheet output**, with the reasoning in §1.8. The blocker is a design large
  enough to test it, not the machinery.
* **Buses**, likewise — deferred until a design has a group wider than two nets.
* **`led-blinker` and `routing-demo` grew on the page.** Both are six- and
  seven-component designs where a left-to-right flow is inherently wider than a
  square grid. The layout does not yet consider wrapping a very tall rank into two
  sub-columns, which is what would fix it and what a fifty-component design will
  need anyway.
* **Freerouting's own violation count is not reconciled with KiCad's DRC.** On
  `mcu-4layer` it reported 19 violations against its own model and the imported
  copper passed DRC with zero. Both numbers are probably right about different
  things; nothing here investigates which.
* **A reviewer still cannot trace a drawn conductor** from one end of a net to the
  other, by design (ADR 0003). Whether a hybrid — short direct wires within a
  cluster, labels between clusters — is worth the correctness risk is an open
  question with a real answer on both sides.
