### The acceptance boards

**`examples/enclosure`** is the shaped-board case: a seven-vertex outline with one
arc (a radiused south-east corner) and a cut-away north-east corner, one rectangular
cutout with a `reason:`, a USB Micro-B fixed at the west edge (`rot: 270`), two fixed
mounting holes, an edge-constrained button and a region-constrained LED. It builds,
places all eleven courtyards inside the real polygon and clear of the cutout
(asserted directly, not assumed), routes all 32 connections, and passes DRC with
zero violations at the source's own `edge_clearance: 0.3`.

**`examples/qfn-fanout`** is the density case: an ATmega328P in a 32-pin QFN on a
0.5 mm pitch. All **16 connected pads** are escaped by the generator (the 17 pins
the design leaves unconnected get no fanout, which is the correct answer for them);
the exposed pad gets a via straight through it; the router then routes between the
escape terminals on B.Cu. 41 connections, 28 vias, 0 DRC violations, byte-stable.

**`examples/overconstrained`** is the hand-over case: four wires that must cross in
one channel on one layer — impossible by planarity. One connection is routed and
**three are handed over** as `unrouted: over_complexity`, each naming the corridor
that blocked it (layer, position, width in mm, demand in mm) and the nets contesting
it. Everything that *is* routed is DRC-clean; three ratlines remain.

### Migration

All seven pre-M9 examples migrated to a `board:` rect block and rebuild
**byte-identically** — verified by comparing against the golden files, and separately
by a test that builds the same design twice, once with the old `layout.outline` and
once with the new `board:` block, and asserts the emitted `Edge.Cuts` graphics are
identical down to the UUIDs. That is what the ring canonicalisation is for: a ring
is emitted from its lowest `(x, y)` corner in KiCad coordinates, wound so the signed
area is positive, which for a Y-up rectangle reproduces exactly the corner order
this tool has written since M3.

### Test coverage

`tests/test_mechanical.py` — 66 tests, including:

* the **sign-convention regression guard**: a board whose north edge is notched and
  whose south edge is straight, so a flipped Y conversion cannot produce a plausible
  answer, plus a fixed part that must land at a specific KiCad coordinate;
* one test per M9c conflict class, including the conservative-bound warning and the
  part-over-cutout case;
* a group deforming around a fixed anchor rather than moving it;
* a cutout separating two corridors, with the two ways round it shown to be
  different crossing sequences;
* the BGA dog-bone unit test — quadrant escape directions and per-pad-instance
  keying, with two pads deliberately sharing a number;
* `sync-placement` round-trip: nudge in KiCad → sync → rebuild → the position is the
  nudged one, the drift is gone, comments and `reason:` survive, and building again
  is byte-identical;
* the hand-over report's shape, and a hand-routed handed-over net surviving a
  rebuild and being routed around on the next run.

## Decisions, and why

Full record in [ADR 0008](../decisions/0008-mech-placement.md). The load-bearing
ones:

**The source frame is Y-up and the conversion lives in one place.** `compile/frame.py`
is the only code that knows KiCad's Y points down. The M7 postmortem's lesson was
that a sign convention nobody tested is a bug waiting for a board revision, so the
asymmetric-outline test exists specifically to make a flipped conversion fail.

**Emitting the export was the small part; propagation was the milestone.** The
router's `board_outline` used to be the *convex hull* of every `Edge.Cuts` point —
conservative for a rectangle, wrong for a concave board, and blind to holes. It is
now a real ring reconstruction, and a cutout becomes a hole in each layer's free
space rather than an obstacle inside it. That distinction is what makes two points
either side of a slot genuinely different homotopy classes; the topology model
needed no change, only to be told about the hole.

**`fixed` outranks a hand-nudge, and `sync-placement` is the other half of that
bargain.** A `fixed:` placement is mechanical law, so `aipcb build` puts a moved part
back and says so; when the board is right and the YAML is stale, `sync-placement`
rewrites the YAML in place, keeping every comment. The source stays the single
truth, and the tool helps update it rather than silently picking a side.

**Fanout is a pattern generator whose output is fixed obstacles plus terminals.**
The generator runs before routing and publishes escape vias as the terminals the
router sees in place of the package's pads; the rubber-band router never learns a
fanout happened. This is also the concrete answer to why an external autorouter is
*rejected* rather than deferred — recorded as such in ADR 0008 so it is not
relitigated.

**Refusal is a first-class outcome.** What cannot be laid DRC-clean is handed over,
never squeezed in. The acceptance bar does not move: zero DRC violations on whatever
*is* routed, plus a machine-readable list of what is not.

## Defects found

Four bugs surfaced during the work, each because a new example was the first board
to exercise the case. All four are fixed; all four are recorded in ADR 0008 under
"What else had to move", because none was in the milestone's scope.

* **Schematic cells were a fixed 50.8 × 45.72 mm.** Every pin gets a stub and a
  label, and a 33-pin MCU symbol is 76 mm tall — so its pins landed on the row
  below's stubs, and KiCad, which connects by exact coordinate, joined them. VCC
  shorted to GND and ERC said so. Cells now grow to fit the biggest symbol on the
  sheet and never shrink below the old figure, so every design of ordinary parts
  lays out exactly as before.
* **Two barrels through one piece of copper.** An escape via a route continues from
  is recorded by both the escape and the route; the router also occasionally landed
  its own via a tenth of a millimetre from one. Both are a fabricator being asked to
  drill twice through the same copper. Each hole is now emitted once, and same-net
  vias whose holes physically overlap are merged onto the earlier one — safe because
  the destination already holds a legal via of the same net and size.
* **Unnumbered pads had UUIDs nothing could map back to source.** A QFN's paste-only
  sub-pads carry no pad number, and the reverse index only covered numbered ones —
  so the standing "every UUID we emit maps back to source" test failed on the new
  example. The index now mirrors the writer's fallback exactly.
* **KiCad's board minimums contradicted the source.** A 0.5 mm-pitch package cannot
  be escaped with KiCad's default 0.5 mm minimum via and 0.3 mm minimum drill, so a
  design whose net classes ask for something tighter now writes those minimums into
  `.kicad_pro` — and only those, so a board built to comfortable rules keeps a
  byte-identical project file. Same reasoning as `edge_clearance`: the router and DRC
  should check one number, not two that happen to agree.

## Deliberate deviations from the specification

One change of behaviour that the milestone did not ask for, flagged because a reader
would otherwise be surprised: **`aipcb check` now routes before running DRC**
(`--no-route` restores the old behaviour). A DRC pass over a board with no copper on
it has checked almost nothing, and `aipcb check --json` cannot list handed-over nets
— which the specification's acceptance requires — without routing. The cost is
runtime: the suite went from 8 m 13 s to 10 m 58 s.

## Deliberately not built

Recorded in [`docs/roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md):

* **MCAD import** (`aipcb import-mech` reading DXF/STEP into `fixed:` blocks). The
  YAML is shaped so this becomes a pure generator later; nothing is built.
* **3D clearance and height checking** against an enclosure model.
* **Back-side placement mirroring** — still validated, warned, and placed on the
  front.
* **Panelization** — mouse bites, V-cut, rails. Fab-level, and a different object
  from a board.
* **Further pattern generators** — differential-pair via transitions with return
  vias, crystal routing, antenna feeds. M9e establishes the architecture; these are
  its next tenants.
* **External autorouters** — rejected, not deferred, for the three reasons in ADR
  0008: determinism, source mapping, preserve semantics.

Two known defects were recorded rather than fixed, both because fixing them was out
of the milestone's guardrails:

* the `diff-pair` copper sliver, which needs stretcher work the guardrails ruled
  out;
* **pads that share a number share a UUID.** A USB Micro-B has twelve pads numbered
  6 and every bundled example has some. KiCad opens and checks such a board, but a
  DRC violation on one of them cannot say which. Fixing it changes the UUID of every
  affected pad, which would break the byte-identical-rebuild promise this milestone
  was asked to keep — so it wants a milestone of its own.

## Open questions carried forward

* **Copper pours** (M10) are what an exposed pad's thermal vias should land in.
  Today they drill into a layer with nothing on it.
* **A differential pair whose pads are fanned out** is routed as two ordinary nets:
  pair detection reads the package's own pads, and those are no longer terminals by
  the time it runs. A pair leaving a fine-pitch package wants a pattern of its own.
* **The conservative-bound placement check** speaks only when its interval bound
  already proves impossibility. Silence means nothing either way; an exact answer
  needs a solver.
* **`via_count` in the fanout generator** models "one via carries about as much
  current as a track as wide as its barrel". That is a rule of thumb standing in for
  a thermal simulation, and it is stated rather than hidden.

## Provenance note

**M9 has no commit of its own.** It landed together with M8 in `aa0b516`
("M8+M9: multilayer routing, and the board's mechanical boundary"). M9 was built
directly on top of M8 while M8 was still uncommitted, and the two are interleaved
inside the same functions rather than merely in the same files, so no honest split
was available — that commit's message states the evidence and records the boundary
in prose instead. See [`m8.md`](m8.md) for the same note from the other side.

When this report was first written, `aa0b516` did not yet exist and the work was in
the working tree only.
