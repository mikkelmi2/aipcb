## 1. What was learned

### 1.1 The headline: the question three milestones have been asking was aimed at the wrong function

M16 measured that 83–94 % of routing time is in the `tighten` stage. Everything
since has reasoned from it — M17c's whole brief, and M18's own. The stage figure is
correct. The inference drawn from it was not.

> **`funnel.py::tighten` costs 0.039 profiled seconds of a 30-second route.**
> 0.13 %. The whole sketch-to-geometry path — `portal_path`, `reduce_crossings`,
> `orient_portals`, `tighten`, `_dedupe`, `stretch_guided` — is under one second.

The `tighten` *stage* is 15.035 s of `pcie-sata`'s 16.196 s of routing, and almost
none of it is tightening. It is building, and then discarding, the free space, the
triangulation and the via sites that tightening is performed against.

### 1.2 The answer to M17c's open question

The brief asked for (a) algorithm replacement with a named algorithm, (b) an
incremental or lazy re-tightening architecture, or (c) a compiled kernel. The answer
is **(b), with a correction to its noun, and with the order of work inverted from
what the framing implied.**

**(a) is rejected, on measurement rather than on argument.** Hershberger &
Snoeyink's paper — obtained in full text from the author's own copy — proves
Theorem 3.2: the Euclidean shortest path homotopic to a given path α is computable
in *O*(*C*<sub>α</sub> + Δ<sub>α</sub>) time, linear in the sleeve. Bespamyatnikh
(2003, also read in full) records that bound as worst-case optimal. That is exactly
what `funnel.py` implements and claims. There is no better algorithm, and §1.1 says
there would be nothing to win if there were.

**(c) is rejected as posed — and the profile says constant-factor work is not
exhausted after all.** "Compile the current algorithm" is the wrong shape: the
largest single cost is `constrained_delaunay_triangles` at 4.30 s, which is GEOS,
which is C already. But the profile found something M17c missed. `locate_many`
batches the R-tree query and then runs a **scalar Python loop**: `_inside` executes
**3 140 252 times** and `_sign` **9 426 354 times** on one board. M17c vectorised
everything around this function and stopped one function short of it.

**(b) is accepted as the structural direction, and four independent sources agree
on it.** Hershberger & Snoeyink's Theorem 3.4 (the funnel can be *maintained*
on-line in the same bound); Shewchuk & Brown 2015 (segment insertion in expected
time linear in the edges crossed); Kallmann, Bieri & Thalmann 2003 (fully dynamic
CDT with insertion *and* removal, measured at roughly 540:1 rebuild-to-update on
their largest data set); and **Dayan's own thesis, §2.2.1**, which says SURF
maintained a per-layer mesh with an incremental CDT, and says why.

**But "incremental re-tightening" is the wrong noun.** Nothing needs to re-tighten;
tightening is free. What needs to stop being rebuilt is the free space, its
triangulation and the via sites — and the largest single cumulative cost in the
profile is not `geometry_for` at all. It is `_repair`, at **16.30 s for sixteen
connections**, building a whole-board field so that one connection can find a
corridor between two pads millimetres apart. That is ordinary engineering with no
new geometry, and it comes before the incremental mesh.

### 1.3 Dayan's 1997 thesis, obtained

The toporouter postmortem's largest gap is closed. **161 pages, in full** — from an
Internet Archive snapshot dated 2017-07-18 of a Semantic Scholar PDF the live site
no longer serves. Every live route is still shut: ProQuest wants an institution,
Semantic Scholar returns an empty page, Google Books has metadata only. The second
attempt succeeded by archaeology rather than by access, and the copy could disappear
again.

Five findings, four of which change something:

1. **The shortest-path algorithm is *O*((*T*+*S*)² log(*T*+*S*)), and the one that
   shipped is not shortest.** SURF used a reduced graph at
   *O*((*T*+*S*) log(*T*+*S*)) that "is guaranteed to find a planar path if one
   exists but the path found may be not a shortest path". This sharpens rather than
   contradicts the postmortem's reading of the proof-versus-heuristic gap.
2. **SURF maintained an incremental CDT per layer** (§2.2.1), explicitly "to improve
   the efficiency of rubber-band updating". This is M17c's open question, answered
   by the original system, in one sentence, in 1997.
3. **C3, the detour pass, is re-priced downward from a primary source.** The roadmap
   cited Blake's 7–16 % on two boards. Dayan measures the same mechanism (his ROAR
   optimiser) on ten two-layer bins and 427 branches: detour **8.84 % → 5.18 %**,
   "the wire length was reduced by about 3.4 %". Combined with M17's finding that 53
   of 55 collapsible spans on this corpus were unimprovable, 3.4 % for a +30 %
   runtime budget no longer looks like a good trade — and that is now the owner's
   call rather than a silent assumption.
4. **C3 gains a structural argument it did not have.** §6.1's *triangle problem*:
   three symmetrical branches whose optimal embedding cannot be produced by routing
   one at a time along shortest paths, **in any order**. A post-pass is therefore
   necessary rather than merely nice.
5. **C5, pairwise-conflict ordering, loses further ground.** Dayan formulates the
   identical mechanism, claims for it only that it beats *shortest-first* (which
   aipcb does not do), and leaves its complexity an open question. And the triangle
   problem says ordering cannot fix what ordering cannot fix.

### 1.4 Two measurements that the survey needed and did not have

**M3 — how much of the free space actually changes between rebuilds.** `geometry_for`
was wrapped from outside and asked, per call, how many obstacles were new since the
previous call for the same layer:

| Board | Layer | calls | obstacles | triangles | new per call, median | disappearances | distinct rule pairs |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| pcie-sata | F.Cu | 137 | 14 → 516 | 683 → 1093 | **2** | 84 | 6 |
| pcie-sata | B.Cu | 39 | 0 → 501 | 192 → 268 | **9** | 12 | 4 |
| mcu-4layer | F.Cu | 56 | 0 → 160 | 414 → 655 | **1** | 8 | 3 |
| qfn-fanout | F.Cu | 24 | 0 → 176 | 484 → 686 | **4** | 0 | 2 |

**A thousand-triangle triangulation is rebuilt 137 times because a median of two
obstacles out of five hundred changed.** Two caveats were measured rather than
assumed, and both constrain any incremental design: the sequence is **not monotone**
(84 obstacles *disappear* from F.Cu across the run), and there are up to **six
distinct `(clearance, track_width)` rule pairs** on one layer, so an incremental
structure needs several meshes per layer rather than one.

**The profiling caveat, stated because it changes how the table above is read.** The
same run takes 19.3 s wall and 37.4 s under `cProfile`, over 92.6 million calls. The
instrument roughly doubles the run and does not do so evenly: Python functions with
millions of calls are inflated, single calls into GEOS barely are. Every figure in
the survey says which side of that line it falls on, and every budget in the M19
draft is stated against `bench`'s unprofiled `router_seconds`.

### 1.5 The congestion lineage: a disagreement worth having read

Two branches contradict each other. BoxRouter 2.0 and NTHU-Route keep PathFinder's
history term and add stabilisers; **FastRoute rejects it outright** — "negotiation
based cost adjustment lacks theoretical basis and requires significant tuning" — and
moves the accumulated state out of the cost and into the *capacity*.

Two things follow for aipcb, and one of them is a compliment.

* **The instability BoxRouter 2.0 documents cannot happen here.** It observed its own
  router "spin out of control" once history came to dominate present cost. aipcb's
  over-capacity cost is a hard 1e6 cliff, and history grows by at most `4 × overuse`
  mm over at most twelve iterations, so it cannot come within five orders of
  magnitude of the cliff. That is structural, not lucky, and it is worth having
  written down.
* **On the one point where all three papers have the same gap, aipcb is already on
  the right side of it.** BoxRouter 2.0's history increment is `+i`, NTHU-Route's is
  `+1`; neither is adaptive to *how much* an edge overflows. aipcb's is
  `history_mm × overuse` — proportional to the overflow, in millimetres. On a grid
  with integer capacities the distinction barely exists; in a real-valued model it
  is the obvious right answer.

Everything else in §2 of the survey is gated: **negotiation costs 1.25 s on the
largest board and ten of eleven examples converge in one iteration**, so no
congestion candidate can justify itself until the congestion stress board exists.

### 1.6 Learned and GPU routing: rejected, and the literature supplies the grounds

The standing hypothesis survives. What changed is that it is now an argument.

* **The field's default evaluation protocol presupposes stochasticity.** PCBWorld
  (2026) — built on KiCad 9.0.8, benchmarked against Freerouting on 679 real boards,
  the most on-topic paper found — reports best-of-five rollouts and 4-seed means as
  its default, and applies seed-averaging **even to Freerouting**.
* **Non-determinism is structural, not a flag.** Liao et al.'s DQN takes random
  actions with probability 0.05 *at solve time*. Cheng et al. document Google's
  Circuit Training diverging and converging on the same machine, environment,
  netlist and seed.
* **The quality case is not made.** PCBWorld's own abstract says learning methods
  "still lag behind rule-based routers". Liao's Attention Routing is 100× faster and
  worse. At MLCAD 2023 the top six teams used no ML.
* **GPU routing buys its speed by accepting races.** InstantGR (ICCAD 2024)
  explicitly uses non-exact overlap checking, "useful for routing algorithms that are
  insensitive to a little overlap".

**The one shape that survives in principle** — a frozen learned ranker inside a
deterministic search — exists in theory (Focal Search: admissible heuristic on OPEN
for the bound, arbitrary learned function on FOCAL for the speed) and has never been
run on PCB routing with a determinism guarantee. It is rejected here anyway on two
further grounds: it would optimise the 7 % of the profile that is search, and **no
paper found measures whether a frozen inference-only model reproduces bit-exactly**.
Three reopening conditions are written out in the survey's §3.5.

### 1.7 Two unexpected findings, both from §3, both useful to §1

* **Determinism under parallelism is a solved problem, by non-ML work.** SPRoute 2.0
  gets deterministic parallel routing from bulk-synchronous batching at a measured
  "< 0.1 %" quality cost; Shen et al. get the stronger *serial equivalency* — the
  same answer as the single-threaded router whatever the core count — at 19.13× on
  32 cores. **If parallelism is ever proposed here, serial equivalency is the bar**,
  because the golden files pin the serial answer.
* **The riskiest candidate is less risky than it was in 2026.** gCDT reports that
  fixed-precision **integer** arithmetic gives deterministic orientation and
  in-circle decisions — and aipcb already snaps every triangulation vertex to
  `_QUANT = 1e-6` mm, so its coordinates are already on an integer lattice. And
  Livesu et al. (TVCG 2022) supply an **optimal deterministic linear-time** cavity
  re-triangulation for segment insertion, with a correctness proof and exact
  orientation predicates, "trivial to implement". That does not reverse ADR 0006; it
  is the evidence a future ADR would have to weigh.

## 4. What the owner needs to decide

The survey session stopped at a draft by design.
[`m19-DRAFT.md`](../milestones/m19-DRAFT.md) proposes, in order: **M19a** vectorise
the point-location tail (budget −10 % corpus `router_seconds`, all eleven board
hashes byte-identical); **M19b** bound the private repair field (−25 % further,
completion and copper unchanged); **M19c** an incremental free-space mesh
(−30 % further, byte-identical hashes, **its own ADR**), explicitly conditional on
the first two not already taking more than half the runtime; and **M19d** re-fit the
extrapolation, where the interesting question is whether the exponents move this
time — M17c's constant-factor work left them exactly where they were.

Five decisions, restated from the draft:

1. **Is M19c in or out?** It is the only item in this project's history that would
   partially reverse an ADR and put a hand-owned geometric predicate into the
   router — the exact failure that killed the gEDA toporouter. The survey recommends
   gating it on M19a and M19b's measured results rather than deciding now.
2. **Does the congestion stress board come before or after?** Every §2 candidate is
   blocked on it and it is already a graduation condition. If it comes first, M19
   shrinks to M19a/M19b.
3. **Does the `pcie-sata` fab round still gate this?** M19a is hash-preserving by its
   acceptance criterion and is arguably exempt; M19b and M19c are not.
4. **Does C3 survive its re-pricing?** ~3.4 % copper for a +30 % runtime budget.
5. **Is a second attempt worth making on the three closed sources?** Only Lu's
   thesis would change M19c's design, and only marginally.
